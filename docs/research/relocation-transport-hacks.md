## 0. The verdict, up front

**The reframe was right about the money and wrong about the risk.** Paul's Nullarbor nostalgia does not need a cost excuse — at cheapest configuration the drive costs roughly what flying costs, and buys five days of trip instead of four hours of transit. On a pure Day-cost basis it is arguably the *better* buy. That is not the problem.

The problem is that **26–30 Dec 2026 is the single worst week of the trip to spend on the Eyre Highway**, for three reasons that are each independently sufficient:

| # | The blocker | The fact |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The listing probably won't exist** | Perth→east is the thinnest relocation corridor in the country. Live snapshot 27 Aug 2026: **4 relocations out of Perth, of which 1 goes east** (Perth→Adelaide). Zero Perth→Melbourne, zero Perth→Sydney. Sydney had **17**. Listings appear **2–4 weeks ahead** — i.e. you'd be waiting until ~5–12 Dec to know, which is *after* the 1 Oct book-by date for the PER→SYD flight. |
| 2 | **The contract doesn't fit the window** | THL (Apollo/Britz/maui/Mighty) publishes **PER→SYD: minimum 8 days, maximum 9**. PER→MEL is min 7. The window from 26 to 30 Dec is **5 days**. A relocation contract for that corridor is not written to be run in five days, and the vehicle is GPS-tracked with a **night-driving ban**. |
| 3 | **Zero buffer meets a road that closes** | The Eyre Highway is a single sealed road with **no detour**. It has closed for bushfire or flood in **Jan 2026 (Caiguna fire, 700 km closed Norseman↔Border Village, >100 trucks and tourists stranded), Feb 2024 (3 days), Mar 2024 (flood, several days), and 2020 (12 days)**. Late December is peak dry-lightning season. NYE in Sydney is a hard Anchor with no Buffer day behind it. |

**Recommended:** fly PER→SYD as planned (26 Dec red-eye, book by 1 Oct — see `domestic-flights.md`), and **scratch the itch on the east coast in January**, where the relocation market is 4× denser, the corridors are 1–3 days long, and a lost day costs a Buffer day rather than the New Year.

**But do not close the door.** Set the imoova/Transfercar waitlists for Perth→Adelaide/Melbourne now, and if a listing with a **19–22 Dec pickup** appears in early December, it is a genuinely good trip: leave a week early, drive it properly at 500–600 km/day, and be in Sydney on the 29th with a day in hand. That version of the drive is the one worth wanting. The 26 Dec version is the same drive with the pleasure taken out and the risk left in.

**Full-fat romance option, priced honestly:** the Indian Pacific departs Perth **every Saturday**, and **26 December 2026 is a Saturday**, arriving Sydney Wednesday 30 Dec 12:30 — a perfect fit for the window. It costs **A$6,800–10,200 per couple** in a Gold Twin. That is 7–10× the flight and 30–50% of the entire couple's Budget. It is the single most expensive way to answer this question.

## 1. How a relocation actually works

The model is simple: rental fleets drift out of balance seasonally (vans pile up in Cairns and Darwin after the dry, in Hobart after summer, in Perth after the WA season). Moving them by transporter costs the operator hundreds; letting a traveller drive one costs them a nominal hire plus a fuel allowance. You get a near-free vehicle on someone else's schedule and route.

**What you actually pay and receive** (composite of imoova, Transfercar and THL's published terms):

| Item | Reality |
|---|---|
| **Daily hire** | **A$1/day** typical; A$5/day on some; occasional A$25/day if posted months out |
| **Booking fee** | Non-refundable admin fee to the platform (amount varies by listing; imoova does not publish a flat figure) |
| **Days allowed** | Fixed by route. imoova shows it as e.g. "**2+3 days**" = 2 base days plus 3 purchasable extra days |
| **Extra day** | THL: **A$75/day fixed**, +250 free km, subject to availability, must be requested at booking or pickup |
| **Km allowance** | THL: **route distance + 550 km**, e.g. PER→SYD 4,550 km, SYD→MEL 1,220 km. Overage **A$0.55/km** |
| **Fuel** | **You pay for it.** Some listings reimburse against receipts: Transfercar advertises **A$350 or a ferry spot**; imoova says "up to A$750" on some listings. **Tax invoices only — EFTPOS/credit-card receipts are explicitly not accepted** |
| **Security deposit / bond** | **A$1,000**, Visa or Mastercard credit *or debit*, **no Amex**, must be in the primary driver's name. 2% debit / 3.1% credit surcharge, refunded with the bond |
| **Standard liability (excess)** | **A$1,000 per claim** — *per claim, not per rental* |
| **Excess reduction** | THL **BP1: A$12/day → A$500**; **BP2: A$20/day → A$250** (Transfercar's copy of the THL terms says BP2 also covers one windscreen and one tyre — see §9, this is the clause that matters most on the Nullarbor) |
| **Pickup / dropoff** | THL relocations: collect **10:00–15:00 only**, return by **15:00** on the last day |
| **Late return** | **A$150** after 15:00; **A$1,000** after 16:00 |
| **Cleaning** | **A$299** if not returned clean (includes smoking and animal smells; no pets except guide dogs) |
| **Claim admin** | **A$75 per claim regardless of fault** |
| **Cancellation by you** | THL **A$50**; imoova refunds nothing after the supplier confirms |
| **Cancellation by them** | Refund of fees only. **No consequential loss** — no flights, no hotels, no compensation |

**The four rules that quietly govern the drive** (THL relocation terms, 1 Apr 2026 – 31 Mar 2027):

1. **No night driving.** "after sunset and before sunrise, except in cities and towns where street lighting is present." Damage at night = **you pay the full cost**, no liability cap.
2. **No unsealed roads.** Full cost for any damage. (The Eyre Highway is sealed end to end, so this is survivable — it rules out side trips.)
3. **GPS speed monitoring.** thl "may use Global Positioning System (GPS) tracking… and will provide warnings if it is identified that you continue to exceed the designated speed limit."
4. **thl may cancel.** "the vehicle model booked or the booking itself may change prior to departure or be cancelled if the circumstances of business needs change."

Rules 1 and 4 are what make this a bad instrument for hitting a hard date.

**What this tells you:**

- **The east coast is the market.** Sydney alone had 4× Perth's inventory, on short 2–4 day corridors.
- **Perth is a net *destination* in late winter** (Broome→Perth, Esperance→Perth), not a source. Perth→east appears, but as a trickle and mostly to Adelaide.
- **Cars and utes outnumber campervans** on these boards now. That matters: a ute or compact car is cheaper to fuel than a motorhome, but gives you no bed, so the Nullarbor becomes A$135–190/night roadhouse rooms instead of A$37 powered sites.
- **The "N+M days" format** (e.g. "4+3") means the base allowance plus purchasable extra days. SYD→BNE at 4+3 is genuinely relaxed. PER→SYD has no such listing to compare.

**Seasonal logic for December:** the fleet flow that would help is real — vans get pulled out of WA and the Top End and pushed to Melbourne/Sydney/Hobart for the summer season. But that repositioning happens in **September–November**, ahead of the season, not during it. By late December the vans are already east and are being churned on short east-coast hires. **December Perth→east relocations are the tail of a migration that has already finished.**

## 4. The nostalgic corridor: Perth → Sydney, 26–30 Dec 2026

### 4.1 The route and the arithmetic

Fastest sealed line is Perth → Norseman → Eyre Highway → Ceduna → Port Augusta → Barrier Highway → Broken Hill → Sydney.

| Segment | km | Driving time |
|---|---|---|
| Perth → Norseman | 722 | ~7 h 45 |
| Norseman → Ceduna (the Nullarbor proper) | 1,201 | ~12 h 20 |
| Ceduna → Port Augusta | 470 | ~4 h 55 |
| Port Augusta → Broken Hill | ~415 | ~4 h 15 |
| Broken Hill → Sydney | 1,151 | ~13 h 00 |
| **Total** | **~3,960 km** | **~42 h** |

Five calendar days (26, 27, 28, 29, 30 Dec) = **~790 km and ~8 h 25 of pure wheel time per day**, before fuel, food, or a single photograph. Add stops and it is **10–11 hours door to door, five days running**. And you *lose three hours* of clock going east (AWST +8 → ACDT +10:30 → AEDT +11), so the fifth day is effectively short.

**A workable staging plan** — this is the honest minimum-misery version of a 5-day crossing:

| Day | Leg | km | Overnight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fri 26 Dec | Perth → **Norseman** | 722 | Norseman motel/caravan park |
| Sat 27 Dec | Norseman → **Eucla / Border Village** | ~720 | Eucla A$160–180 room, or powered site |
| Sun 28 Dec | Eucla → **Port Augusta** | ~960 | The hard day. Time zone shifts twice |
| Mon 29 Dec | Port Augusta → Broken Hill → **Nyngan/Cobar** | ~865 | |
| Tue 30 Dec | Nyngan → **Sydney** | ~700 | Arrive late afternoon |

Two things about that table. First, days 3 and 4 are ~10 hours of driving each and there is nothing to look at for most of it — this is the version of the Nullarbor that produces the memory of *having done it*, not the memory of *doing it*. Second, it assumes **two licensed drivers** (see §10 on the IDP).

**Heat.** Milder than the reputation, on the coastal half. Nullarbor/Eucla December average max is **~32 °C**, January **~33 °C**; Ceduna averages **27–29 °C**. Those are sea-breeze numbers. The dangerous parts are the inland shoulders: the Goldfields run out of Perth, and the Port Augusta → Broken Hill → Cobar stretch, which is genuine 40 °C+ country. And the averages hide the tail — **Ceduna hit 49.5 °C on 26 January 2026** and Eucla 49.8 °C on 19 December 2019. Heat strategy is the standard one: drive the morning, take the middle of the day at a roadhouse if it spikes, carry **4–5 L of water per person per day** independent of the vehicle, and never treat air-conditioning as a plan.

**Daylight is generous, and you need all of it.** Around the solstice you get roughly 14 hours between sunrise and sunset along the whole route — enough for a 790 km day inside the night-driving ban, but only if you are rolling at first light every single morning. Which collides with the other rule: **dawn and dusk are peak kangaroo hours**, and a large roo at 110 km/h writes off roughly one in five vehicles it hits. You are being pushed into the two most dangerous hours of the day by the schedule.

**Fuel and roadhouses.** Twelve stops between Norseman and Ceduna; longest gaps **191 km** (Norseman→Balladonia) and **187 km** (Border Village→Nullarbor Roadhouse). Not heroic, but assume nothing: hours vary, deliveries are infrequent, and remote telecoms fail — **carry a cash backup**. Live prices at Nullarbor Roadhouse (Aug 2026): **ULP91 A$2.95/L, PULP95 A$3.04, diesel A$3.24**, against a Perth metro average of **A$1.94–2.07/L**. Roadhouse rooms run **A$135–190/night** (Balladonia A$135–190, Eucla ~A$160–180, Nullarbor Roadhouse ~A$141–149); a **powered site is A$37**.

### 4.2 Can a relocation vehicle even do it?

**No, not comfortably, and probably not at all in that window.** THL publishes minimum and maximum relocation days per city pair (their own terms document, 1 Apr 2026 – 31 Mar 2027):

| Corridor | Km allowance | Min days | Max days |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PER → SYD** | 4,550 | **8** | **9** |
| PER → MEL | 3,900 | 7 | 8 |
| PER → ADL | 3,150 | 6 | 7 |
| PER → BNE | 5,150 | 10 | 11 |
| ADL → SYD | 1,800 | 4 | 5 |
| ADL → MEL | 1,100 | 3 | 4 |
| MEL → SYD | 1,220 | 3 | 4 |
| SYD → BNE | 1,350 | 3 | 4 |
| SYD → CNS | 3,400 | 6 | 7 |
| MEL → HBT | 600 | 4 | 5 |

The km allowance is fine (4,550 vs 3,960 actual — 590 km of slack). The **days are not**: the corridor is written as an 8–9 day job. You would be running a contractually 8-day relocation in 5 days, at 790 km/day, in a GPS-monitored vehicle, under a night-driving ban, with a A$1,000 penalty for arriving after 16:00 on the final day. Nothing about that is illegal; everything about it removes the pleasure.

**And the listing has to exist.** Zero PER→SYD relocations were listed on 27 Aug 2026, and the corridor does not appear in the market's normal flow. The realistic best case is **PER→ADL** (which does list) plus a separate ADL→SYD or ADL→MEL leg — but chaining two relocations across Christmas week, each posted 2–4 weeks ahead, each cancellable by the operator, is a plan with two independent single points of failure stacked in front of a hard Anchor.

### 4.3 What a lost day costs

This is the number that decides it.

| Failure | Probability | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Eyre Highway closed** (bushfire/flood) | Documented in Jan 2026, Feb 2024, Mar 2024, 2020. Late Dec is peak dry-lightning season. Call it **low single-digit percent for any given 2-day window, but with no detour and no cap on duration** | 1–12 days. The Jan 2026 Caiguna fire closed 700 km and stranded 100+ trucks at Border Village with no rooms left. The 2020 fire closed it for **12 days**. **NYE is gone**, and you are 2,000 km from Sydney with no way out but the road you're on |
| **Operator cancels the relocation** | Explicitly reserved in THL's terms; imoova refunds fees only | Emergency PER→SYD flight inside 3 weeks: **A$800–1,200 pp = A$1,600–2,400/couple** (per `domestic-flights.md`) — i.e. 2× what the pre-booked fare would have been |
| **Mechanical / tyre / windscreen** | Elevated: road trains, 110 km/h, stone chips, heat | You may spend A$100 on minor repairs; anything larger needs thl's **prior written consent**, and thl is explicit that it is **"not liable for any incidental costs (accommodation, transportation)"** from a breakdown |
| **Fatigue-driven single-vehicle incident** | The real risk on 790 km days | **Single-vehicle rollover is excluded from every liability-reduction product.** You pay the full value of the vehicle |
| **Simply running slow** | High. One bad day and the plan is 800 km behind | A$150 late fee, or A$1,000 after 16:00, or a missed NYE |

Against a Buffer-day philosophy this is the one Leg where a buffer cannot exist: NYE in Sydney is date-locked, and the only way to buy insurance is to depart earlier, which is exactly what the 26 Dec Christmas Anchor forbids.

### 4.4 Cost model — cheapest viable configuration

Fuel assumption: blended **A$2.45/L petrol, A$2.70/L diesel** across the route (roughly half the distance at metro/regional prices, half at roadhouse prices — Perth metro A$1.94–2.07, Nullarbor Roadhouse A$2.95/3.24).

**Comparison set:**

| Option | Per couple A$ | € | Days consumed | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fly PER→SYD 26 Dec** | **1,000** | 610 | 0 (red-eye) | Plan-on figure from `domestic-flights.md`; band A$800–1,400. Book by 1 Oct |
| **Relocation campervan drive** | **~1,320** | ~805 | 5 | *If a listing exists.* A$264/day all-in for two — well under the A$500 Daily cap |
| Relocation car + roadhouse rooms | ~1,330 | ~810 | 5 | Same money, less fun |
| Paid one-way rental PER→SYD | 2,500–4,000+ | 1,525–2,440 | 5 | Transcontinental one-way fees are punitive and quotes are route-and-date specific — **get it quoted, never assume** |
| **Indian Pacific, Gold Twin** | **6,800–10,200** | 4,150–6,220 | 5 | §7 |
| Emergency flight if the drive fails | 1,600–2,400 | 975–1,465 | 0 | On top of whatever you already spent |

**Read this honestly:** the drive is **A$320 more expensive than flying, and it delivers five days of travel for that A$320.** Under this trip's own Day-pricing logic — where a Buffer day still costs lodging and food wherever it lands — five days at A$264/couple/day is *cheap living*, cheaper than five days in Sydney. The nostalgia is very nearly free.

**So the cost math works. The logistics don't.** The verdict against 26–30 Dec is not "it isn't worth it"; it's "the vehicle probably won't exist, and if it does, one closed road ends New Year's Eve."

### 4.5 The version of this that *does* work

If the drive matters — and the reframe says it does — buy it a buffer:

- **Leave Perth 19–22 Dec.** Cross at 500–600 km/day, seven days, with a night in Esperance or Kalgoorlie, a proper stop at the Bunda Cliffs, Head of Bight (whale season is June–Oct, so this is scenery not whales), and Streaky Bay. Arrive Sydney 27–29 Dec with a day in hand. This is the trip Paul actually remembers.
- **Cost:** Christmas in Perth is a hard Anchor, so this only works if the family Christmas moves or is skipped. That's a family question, not a research question — **flag it to the Travellers rather than deciding it.**
- **Or:** fly PER→ADL and drive/relocate ADL→SYD (1,800 km, THL min 4 / max 5 days) — you get the Nullarbor's eastern flavour, the Barrier Highway, Broken Hill, and a corridor that actually lists. Adelaide→Melbourne and Adelaide→Sydney relocations were both live in the 27 Aug snapshot.

## 5. The calmer alternative: a relocation Leg later in the trip

This is the recommendation. Same hack, same fun, one-tenth the exposure.

| Corridor | THL km / min–max days | imoova base rate | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SYD → MEL (coastal)** | 1,220 km, 3–4 days | Listed in the 27 Aug snapshot (Coast Hopper 3-berth, "2+3 days") | Sapphire Coast, Lakes Entrance, Ninety Mile Beach, or inland via the Alps. 3–4 days is a real holiday, not an endurance event. Pairs with the Melbourne Capsule |
| **SYD → BNE / Gold Coast / Sunshine Coast** | 1,350 km, 3–4 days | **The densest corridor in the country** — 8 of 17 Sydney listings | Pairs directly with the Byron/Nimbin Capsule. Compact cars at "4+3 days" is generous |
| **MEL → ADL** | 1,100 km, 3–4 days | 3 Adelaide→Melbourne listings live | Great Ocean Road westbound. Reverse direction lists more often — check both |
| **SYD/BNE → CNS** | 3,400 km, 6–7 days | Cairns→Brisbane and Sydney→Cairns both live | Long, hot, and mostly Bruce Highway. Only worth it if the reef Capsule is on and they want the drive |
| **MEL → HBT** | 600 km, 4–5 days | Occasional | **The ferry is the prize** — thl offers "either a fuel allowance or a spot on the ferry." Spirit of Tasmania vehicle fares are expensive; a relocation that includes one is a genuine saving. Book the ferry for a **7 m vehicle** to allow a vehicle-type swap |

**Day allowances vs the Buffer philosophy.** This is the honest tension: a relocation is the *opposite* of a Buffer day. It is date-locked at both ends, GPS-monitored, and penalised at A$150–1,000 for lateness. A 3-day SYD→MEL relocation is three days you cannot flex.

The way to make it compatible: **treat a relocation as a Capsule with a hard Lock, and put a Buffer day immediately after the drop-off, not before it.** Buy the extra days where they're offered — SYD→BNE at "4+3" means A$1/day base and the option of three more; THL extra days are A$75 each, which is worse but still cheap against a rental. And book the relocation **only for a segment where a 1-day slip is absorbable** — which is everywhere on this trip except the NYE Leg and the international departure.

## 6. Private driveaway in 2026: mostly dead

Short answer: **person-to-person "drive my car across the country" is not a functioning market in Australia in 2026.** The pieces that used to make it work have all been removed.

| Thing | 2026 status |
|---|---|
| **Car Next Door → Uber Carshare** | **Dead.** Uber shut the Australian P2P carshare operation on **12 September 2024**, two and a half years after paying ~A$105 m for Car Next Door, citing insurance, theft and repair costs. Uber pivoted to Uber Rent with Thrifty/Budget/Hertz |
| **DriveMyCar** | **Alive but transformed.** Now routes mid-to-long-term hires to **Carly Car Subscription** and short-term to **iDrive Rentals**. Long-term P2P (7–365 days), airport pickups, Uber-driver vehicles. **No driveaway or one-way relocation product.** Not a cross-country option |
| **Turo Australia** | Operating. **One-way rentals are not permitted** — pickup and dropoff must match. Some hosts will negotiate a location change after booking, but this is an exception, not a product, and it is not a cross-country instrument |
| **Upmove / Automover / Allied / CEVA** | Alive and legitimate — but these are **truck transporters**, not driveaway. Perth→Sydney takes **6–9 working days** on a car carrier. Useful only if the Travellers ever buy a car in Perth and want it east |
| **Shiply-style bid boards** | Upmove is the closest Australian analogue. Listings are for transport companies to bid on, not for individuals to drive |
| **Gumtree / Facebook "driver wanted" posts** | These exist and are **the sketchy tier**. No contract, no insurance clarity, no recourse. If someone's private car is damaged or stolen on your watch, you are personally liable and their comprehensive policy may not name you as a driver. **Recommend against.** If ever considered: written agreement, named-driver confirmation in writing from the owner's insurer, photographic condition report at both ends, and no cash changing hands |

**Bottom line for the site:** the only legitimate relocation channel in Australia in 2026 is the **rental-fleet** channel (imoova / Transfercar / Coseats / operator-direct). Everything person-to-person is either gone, round-trip-only, or uninsurable.

- **Sits covering NYE exist and are listed four months out** — earlier than the commonly-quoted "2–3 months." A Rozelle or St Leonards sit would put them in inner Sydney, free, across the entire NYE window. - **But it is a lottery, and they are the worst-positioned entrants.** Sydney and Melbourne summer sits draw **10–20 applications within hours of posting**, from a pool that includes locals and reviewed full-time sitters. A **couple with no sits history and no reviews** is at the bottom of that pile. Realistic odds on a specific inner-Sydney NYE sit: **low — treat it as a bonus, never as the lodging plan.**

Also target **February**, when they'll be somewhere on the east coast anyway and competition has collapsed.

### 8.2 Rideshare / fuel-split

**[Coseats](https://coseats.com/)** is the main Australian carpool board and the same site that carries relocations. Drivers post an interstate run, passengers chip in for fuel; both sides are rated. There is no fixed price — you nominate what you'd pay or be paid.

Its real use here is as a **failure mode absorber, not a plan**: if a relocation lands but only fits one of them, or if they end up in Adelaide with a gap, Coseats is a live board of people driving the same road. It is also the cheapest possible Nullarbor crossing, at nothing but a share of the fuel — which for A$680–1,000 of petrol split three ways is A$230–330 each. Not bookable in advance with any confidence, so it never goes in the Plan; it goes in the back pocket.

The 2000-era hitchhike itself has no 2026 equivalent worth recommending, and the honest thing to say is that this board is the closest thing that exists.

## 9. Insurance, bond and excess — the gotchas, plainly

This is the section that costs money if it's wrong.

### 9.1 The operator's cover

- Standard liability on a relocation is **A$1,000 per claim, not per rental.** Two incidents, two A$1,000 hits.
- **BP1 A$12/day → A$500. BP2 A$20/day → A$250.** On a 5-day Nullarbor run BP2 costs A$100 and is worth it.
- **What no liability-reduction product covers, ever:**
  - **Overhead and underbody damage** — full cost, always.
  - **Single-vehicle rollover** — "the hirer will be liable for the full cost." This is the fatigue/swerve failure mode on the exact road in question.
  - **Damage on unsealed roads** — full cost.
  - **Damage while night driving** — full cost.
  - **Damage from exceeding the vehicle's load rating** — voids all cover.
- **Tyres and windscreens:** thl's general terms say they are not covered. Transfercar's copy of the THL relocation terms says **BP2 covers one windscreen and one tyre**. This contradiction is worth resolving at the counter — on the Nullarbor, behind road trains, **a stone-chipped windscreen is close to the modal claim.** Get it in writing which applies before signing.
- **A$75 administration fee on every claim, regardless of fault.** **A$299 cleaning fee.** **A$150/A$1,000 late fees.**
- **Mechanical breakdown:** you may spend up to A$100 on minor repairs; more needs prior written consent, and thl is explicit that it is **not liable for your accommodation or onward transport**.

### 9.2 Credit-card rental excess cover — verified, and the answer is nuanced

The brief's hypothesis was that credit-card excess cover "likely does NOT apply to A$1 relocation vehicles." **That is not quite right, and the real exclusions are worse than a blanket one.**

Reading the actual policy wording rather than the marketing page — the Westpac Consumer Credit Cards Complimentary Insurance booklet (underwritten by Allianz), which is representative of the major Australian issuers:

> **rental vehicle** — "a campervan/motorhome that does not exceed 4.5 tonnes, a sedan, coupe, hatchback, station-wagon, SUV, four wheel drive or mini bus/people mover rented from a licensed motor vehicle rental company or agency. Rental vehicle does not include any other vehicle irrespective of type or weight."

So:

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Does "relocation" appear as an exclusion? | **No.** Nothing in the wording excludes relocations, one-way hires, or nominal-rate hires |
| Are campervans covered? | **Yes — up to 4.5 tonnes.** A 2–3 berth camper qualifies |
| Are large motorhomes covered? | **No.** A 6-berth motorhome routinely exceeds 4.5 t, putting it outside the definition entirely |
| Is a **dual-cab ute** covered? | **Doubtful.** Utes are not in the list, and the wording closes hard: "does not include any other vehicle irrespective of type or weight." A ute marketed as a 4WD may squeak in; **do not rely on it.** This matters because **the utes are exactly what's listing out of Perth** |
| Is a box truck covered? | **No** |
| Payment condition | "the entire payment for the vehicle rental was charged to the accountholder's card account" — trivially satisfied by a A$1–5 relocation charged to the card. **Charge the hire and the liability-reduction fee to the card** |
| Benefit | Lesser of the damage, the agreement's specified amount, or **A$5,500** — comfortably above a A$1,000 relocation excess. **A$300 claim excess** applies |
| **The exclusion that actually bites** | *"you using the rental vehicle in breach of the rental vehicle agreement."* The relocation agreement bans night driving and unsealed roads. **Break either and you lose the operator's liability cap AND the credit-card cover at the same time** |
| **The second exclusion that bites** | The policy "does not cover items such as, but not limited to, tyres and/or windscreens, roof and underbody **if they are not covered by the indemnity provided by the rental company**." Those are precisely the items the relocation contract carves out. **The Nullarbor's characteristic damage is uncovered on both sides** |

**Three more things, stated plainly:**

1. **This is Australian-card cover.** The Travellers depart from Valencia. If they hold **Spanish/EU cards**, an Australian bank's complimentary insurance is irrelevant to them, and European card CDW products commonly exclude campervans/motorhomes outright and require a standard rental agreement. **Verify with the actual issuer and get the policy wording — never the marketing page.** If they have no valid card cover, **take BP2 at A$20/day**; on a 5-day drive that is A$100 to move A$1,000 of exposure to A$250.
2. **The card and the bond must be in the primary driver's name.** No Amex. Visa/Mastercard credit or debit only. A card in the other partner's name will be refused at the counter.
3. **The bond is A$1,000 held live on the card for the whole hire**, plus a 2–3.1% surcharge that is refunded with it. Make sure the card has headroom on top of everything else the trip is charging.

## 10. The "how to pounce" playbook

Relocations are a **watch-and-strike** instrument, not a booking. Treat it as a standing background process from now until the trip.

**Set up now (September 2026):**

1. **imoova account + waitlists** for every corridor of interest: Perth→Adelaide, Perth→Melbourne, Perth→Sydney, Sydney→Melbourne, Sydney→Brisbane, Melbourne→Adelaide, Melbourne→Hobart. Waitlists alert by **SMS and email**. Install the **imoova app** for push.
2. **Transfercar account** with saved searches on the same corridors. Transfercar blocks scripted access — this one needs a real browser and a real login.
3. **Coseats account** — smaller inventory, no booking fee, and doubles as the carpool fallback.
4. **Email `aurelocsonline@thlonline.com`** to join the Britz/maui/Apollo/Mighty relocation driver database. This is the operator's own list and predates the aggregators.
5. **Aussie House Sitters membership (A$89)** — and start building reviews immediately (§8.1). This has the longest lead time of anything here.

**Lead-time expectations — this is the whole game:**

- Listings appear **2–4 weeks** before the vehicle must move. Britz's own page targets people free "within the next three weeks."
- Rarely, a listing appears months out — usually at a higher rate (~A$25/day) rather than A$1.
- Perks (fuel allowance, extra days) are often **added as the date approaches** and the vehicle hasn't moved. A listing that looks thin at 3 weeks can look generous at 10 days.
- **First-come, first-served.** Supplier confirmation takes up to 48 hours. Decide in minutes, not evenings.
- **The collision with the flight booking curve is the core problem for the Christmas Leg:** PER→SYD must be booked by ~1 Oct to hold A$500 pp, but a 26 Dec relocation won't surface until ~5–12 Dec. There is no way to wait for one without giving up the cheap fare. **Buy the flight; treat any relocation as a bonus you'd walk away from.**

**Documents and kit to have ready before leaving Spain:**

| Item | Why |
|---|---|
| **Full driver's licence, physical card** | Digital licences are not accepted at relocation counters |
| **International Driving Permit** for the non-Australian licence holder | THL requires "a valid Full International licence, accompanied by an accredited English translation or an International driver's permit **and a passport**," as **hardcopy at collection**. **An IDP can only be issued in the country that issued the licence** — Spain's DGT must issue it **before departure**, and it is valid one year. Without it, only one of them can legally drive, which turns a 790 km day from hard into unsafe |
| **Visa or Mastercard (credit or debit) in the primary driver's name** | **No Amex.** Must carry A$1,000 of headroom for the bond plus the surcharge |
| **Passport** | Required alongside a foreign licence |
| **The issuer's actual policy wording** (PDF, downloaded) | To know before the counter whether their card covers a campervan, a ute, or nothing |
| **Cash** | Remote roadhouse card terminals fail |
| **Water: 4–5 L per person per day**, independent of the vehicle | Non-negotiable on the Eyre Highway |

**At the counter — the five questions to ask before signing:**

1. Does BP2 cover **one windscreen and one tyre** on this specific contract? Get it written on the agreement.
2. What is the **exact latest return time** and the late-fee schedule?
3. Are **extra days** available on this vehicle, and at what rate?
4. What is the **fuel allowance**, and what receipt format is required? (Tax invoice with item description — EFTPOS slips are rejected.)
5. Is a **second driver** included at no charge, and is she listed on the agreement?

**Photograph everything at pickup** — roof, underbody, all four tyres, windscreen, every panel, the odometer and the fuel gauge — because roof and underbody are uncovered by every product and are the easiest things to be blamed for.
