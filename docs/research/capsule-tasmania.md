# Capsule: Tasmania — the south-to-north arc, Jan 2027

Research for [issue #14](https://github.com/kilbot/holidays/issues/14). Defines one **Capsule** for the Plan: a route, a duration, a best-of itinerary, and a cost band. First visit, no own car, flying in from Sydney.

**Researched:** 26 August 2026. All source URLs carry an access date in [Sources](#sources).
**Currency:** prices are **AUD** unless stated. Attraction and operator fares are **per adult**, published as at Aug 2026.
**FX rate used: A$1 = €0.61.** EUR figures rounded to the nearest €5.

> **Confidence.** Attraction and park-pass prices are **high confidence** — published rates pulled from the operators and from Parks & Wildlife Service Tasmania (prices last set 1 Jul 2026, so they cover the travel dates unless there is a 1 Jul 2027 rise, which is after the trip). Drive times are **high confidence** but are *map* times; the plan-on times in §6 are padded and are a judgement call. Lodging and car-hire figures are **modelled** from the sibling [cost-baselines.md](./cost-baselines.md) plus Aug-2026 listings; January 2027 rates are not broadly published yet. Weather figures are long-run climate averages, not a forecast. Re-snapshot lodging and car hire in **late September 2026** — earlier than the reef Capsule, because Tasmania's January car fleet sells out (§7).

> **Correction to sibling research.** [domestic-flights.md](./domestic-flights.md) §3 cites "MONA FOMA and the Cygnet Folk Festival" as early-January demand drivers. **MONA FOMA no longer exists** — David Walsh cancelled it in April 2024 after 16 years, and the final edition ran in February 2024. **Falls Festival Marion Bay is also gone** — it left Tasmania permanently in 2021 and last ran at Marion Bay in 2019. Only Cygnet survives from that list. The January event calendar is thinner than the flights doc assumes; the demand peak is school holidays and the Sydney–Hobart, not festivals.

## 2. Which shape: the lap, the arc, or a Hobart base

### Verdict: the open-jaw arc, Hobart → Launceston

**Why it wins for this trip**

- **Tasmania's highlights sit on a diagonal.** Hobart and the Tasman Peninsula are in the far south-east; Freycinet is halfway up the east coast; the Bay of Fires is in the north-east; Launceston is at the top. That is a single, monotonic line — no backtracking, and every driving day under three hours. A Hobart-return itinerary has to come back down that line, and the return leg (St Helens → Hobart, or Launceston → Hobart) is **2h20–3h30 of road you have already earned nothing new from**.
- **The open jaw is free.** Sydney flies nonstop to both Tasmanian airports — SYD–HBA has 20 Qantas / 14 Jetstar / 14 Virgin services a week; SYD–LST has 7 Jetstar / 6 Qantas / 5 Virgin. Returns run **A$130–220 (HBA)** and **A$110–190 (LST)** in the non-peak part of January, so half of each priced as an open jaw lands in the same band as a Hobart return, or slightly under. There is **no fare penalty for flying an open jaw**, only for having fewer LST departures to choose from.
- **The one-way car drop is cheap or free.** Hobart↔Launceston is a balanced route for Tasmanian rental fleets; several majors charge nothing, and where a relocation fee applies it has historically been in the **A$0–150** range rather than the hundreds. Budget **A$55**; verify at booking.
- **Hobart is the right arrival buffer.** It's a city — restaurants, MONA, a market, a mountain — so a delayed flight or a wet first day costs nothing. Arriving into Launceston and immediately driving 2h20 to Cradle Mountain puts the trip's weakest weather bet on its most jet-lagged day.
- **Launceston is a 15-minute departure.** LST airport is close to town, the last night is in a walkable city, and the drive to the gate is trivial.

**What it costs you**

- **Fewer flights out of Launceston.** 18 nonstops a week to Sydney against ~48 from Hobart. A cancellation on the LST leg has fewer same-day alternatives. Buy the earlier departure, not the last one.
- **You cannot pick the car up on the last day.** The one-way arc means you hold the car for the whole trip unless you drop it early in Launceston, which you can (§8 has the "leave the car for the Hobart days" lever).

### The classic lap — ruled out at 9 nights

The lap adds the **west coast**: Strahan, the Gordon River cruise, Queenstown, the Franklin. It is genuinely the wildest part of Tasmania and it is genuinely worth doing — on a second visit, or on a 14-night first visit. Against it here:

- **Hobart → Strahan is 6 hours**, and Strahan → Cradle Mountain another ~2h30. Adding the west coast adds two full driving days plus two nights minimum.
- **It is the wettest corner of the wettest state.** Strahan sits under a temperate rainforest rainfall regime; Cradle Mountain averages ~1,560 mm a year. January improves the odds, it does not fix them.
- **The lap's marginal attraction is a boat trip you have a better version of elsewhere.** The Gordon River cruise is fine; the Tasman Island and Bruny cruises off the south-east coast are, for a first visitor, better boats in better scenery and one-third of the driving.

### A Hobart-only base — ruled out

Hobart alone genuinely fills 4 days (MONA, Salamanca, kunanyi, Bruny, Port Arthur, Richmond, Bonorong). It also delivers none of the images the couple came for. **Wineglass Bay and the Bay of Fires are why people say "Tasmania".** A 4-night Hobart Capsule is a good city break that happens to be on an island; it is not the Tasmania Capsule.

### Reversing the arc — the variant worth knowing

If **Cradle Mountain is non-negotiable**, run the arc **north to south**: fly into Launceston, do Cradle first, then work down the east coast and finish in Hobart. Ending in Hobart makes it easier to land Salamanca Market on the final Saturday and gives you the airport with the most departures for the flight home. The cost: your least-reliable weather day is your first day, with no second shot. See §3 for why that trade is usually a losing one at 9 nights.

## 3. How long — min, ideal, max

The binding constraint here is **not weather** (that's the reef Capsule's problem). It is **transit cost per hub**.

Tasmania's highlights are dispersed over an island roughly 300 km across, on roads where a 100 km/h limit is the exception. Every hub you add costs **half a day of driving plus a minimum of one night** — and hubs that only get one night are hubs you drove to and then left. So the duration question is: *how many hubs can you afford, and which ones earn their transit?*

| Hub | Transit in | Nights needed | What it delivers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hobart** | airport, 15 min | 3–4 | MONA, Salamanca Market, kunanyi, Bruny Island, Port Arthur, food |
| **Tasman Peninsula** | 1h30 from Hobart | 0–1 | Port Arthur, Tasman Island sea cliffs, ghost tour |
| **Freycinet / Coles Bay** | 3h from Hobart | **2** | Wineglass Bay, the Hazards, Cape Tourville |
| **Bay of Fires / St Helens** | 2h from Coles Bay | 1 | The best beach in the Capsule — and it's *on the way* |
| **Launceston** | 2h15–2h45 from St Helens | 1 | Cataract Gorge, Tamar Valley, the departure airport |
| **Cradle Mountain** | 2h20 from Launceston, 4h30–5h from Hobart | **2** | The alpine half of Tasmania — and a coin flip on cloud |

| | Nights | Full days | Hubs | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Floor (do not do this)** | 4 | 3 | 1 (Hobart) | A city break. You flew to Tasmania and did not see Tasmania. |
| **Minimum** | **6** | **5** | **3** | Hobart 3 / Coles Bay 2 / Launceston 1. Gets MONA, one Hobart headline day, and Wineglass Bay. Real, but you will feel the omissions. |
| **Ideal** | **9** | **8** | **5** | Hobart 4 / Tasman Peninsula 1 / Coles Bay 2 / Bay of Fires 1 / Launceston 1. Every one of the consensus must-dos except Cradle Mountain, no driving day over 3 hours, and two slack half-days built in. |
| **Maximum** | **12** | **11** | **7** | The ideal + Launceston 1 + **Cradle Mountain 2**. This is the point at which Cradle stops being a raid and becomes a stay. |
| **Beyond** | 14+ | | | Adds the west coast (Strahan, Gordon River), Maria Island, the Tamar and Tasman Peninsula multi-day walks. A second visit, or a different Plan. |

**Why 9 and not 7.** Seven nights forces one of two bad choices: drop Freycinet to one night (which turns Wineglass Bay from a morning walk into a drive-by), or drop the Tasman Peninsula (which removes the convict half of Tasmania's story). Nights eight and nine are the cheapest in the whole Capsule — roughly A$490 each of lodging and food — and each buys back a whole hub.

**Why not 12 by default.** Twelve nights is not wrong; it is *right and expensive*. It adds ~A$2,100 (€1,280) and three days, almost all of it to buy Cradle Mountain, which in January is the one item on the list you can drive 2h20 to and find in cloud. If the Plan has 12 nights of Tasmania to give, take them. If it is choosing between three more Tasmania nights and a whole other Capsule elsewhere, **take the other Capsule**.

## 4. The ideal version, day by day

Nine nights, arriving **Friday 15 January 2027**. Two dates pin this itinerary and you should build around them, not fight them:

- **Salamanca Market is Saturdays only, 08:30–15:00**, every week of the year.
- **MONA is closed Tuesdays and Wednesdays** (open Thu–Mon, 10:00–17:00).

Arriving on a Friday puts the market on day 2 and MONA on day 3, and pushes the Tuesday out of Hobart onto a driving or Port Arthur day. Any Wednesday/Thursday arrival works too. A **Monday or Tuesday arrival is the one to avoid** — it strands MONA against the closed days.

| Day | Plan | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| **Day 1 — Fri 15 Jan. Arrive Hobart** | Fly SYD→HBA (1h50). **Do not collect a car** — see §8. Check in around Salamanca or Battery Point. Walk Salamanca Place, Kelly's Steps, Battery Point; dinner. | Nothing booked. The arrival buffer is a city, so a late flight costs nothing. |
| **Day 2 — Sat 16 Jan. Salamanca + kunanyi** | **Salamanca Market** 08:30–15:00 (free, 300+ stalls). Afternoon: **kunanyi / Mt Wellington summit** — 1,270 m, 30 min from town by car or the Explorer Bus (~A$35 pp return, 2.5 h). Evening: Hobart's food. | The market is free, unmissable, and only happens on Saturdays — it is the pin the whole week hangs on. kunanyi is a half-day and the best free view in the Capsule; do it on a clear afternoon and be flexible about which one. |
| **Day 3 — Sun 17 Jan. MONA** | **Mona Roma ferry** from Brooke Street Pier (departures 09:15 onward, ~A$30 pp return; book 2 days ahead in summer). Museum A$39 pp. Allow the whole day — the ferry ride is part of it. | The single most distinctive thing in Tasmania and, for a lot of visitors, the reason the island is on the list at all. Sunday is inside MONA's Thu–Mon window and leaves the Tuesday free for the road. |
| **Day 4 — Mon 18 Jan. Bruny Island** | Collect the hire car. Drive to **Kettering** (35 min), **SeaLink ferry** (~A$50.60 return per car, 20 min crossing, passengers free). The Neck lookout, Cape Bruny lighthouse, oysters/cheese/berries at Adventure Bay. *Toggle:* the **Pennicott Bruny Island circumnavigation** instead — A$245 pp, 09:00–17:00 ex-Hobart, no driving. | Bruny is the food-and-coastline day, and self-driving it is a fifth of the guided price. Go early: the Kettering ferry queue can run two sailings deep on a January morning. |
| **Day 5 — Tue 19 Jan. Port Arthur + Tasman Peninsula** | Check out, drive Hobart → Port Arthur (1h30 via Eaglehawk Neck; stop at the Tessellated Pavement and the Neck lookout). **Port Arthur Historic Site** A$55 pp — a 2-consecutive-day ticket including a 20-min harbour cruise and guided talks. **Tasman Island Cruise** A$180 pp, 3 h, the tallest sea cliffs in the Southern Hemisphere. Sleep on the peninsula; **ghost tour** A$35 pp, 18:00 or 20:00. | Tuesday is a MONA-closed day, so it is the cheapest day to be out of Hobart. Sleeping on the peninsula is what makes the ghost tour possible and shortens tomorrow. |
| **Day 6 — Wed 20 Jan. Peninsula → Freycinet** | Drive Port Arthur → Coles Bay, ~3 h via Sorell, Orford and Swansea. Stop at **Devil's Corner** cellar door (the view over the Hazards) and **Kate's Berry Farm**. Arrive early afternoon. **Cape Tourville** lighthouse boardwalk (20 min, easy, huge views). Swim at Honeymoon Bay. | The one three-hour driving day, broken by two of the best roadside stops on the island. The afternoon deliberately holds nothing that has to happen. |
| **Day 7 — Thu 21 Jan. Wineglass Bay** | **Early start.** Wineglass Bay Lookout is 3 km return, 1–1.5 h. Continue to the beach (6 km return, ~2.5 h) or do the **Hazards Beach circuit** (11 km, 4–5 h). Afternoon: Friendly Beaches, or nothing. *Toggle:* **Wineglass Bay Cruise**, 4.5 h, 09:45–14:30, A$210 pp with lunch — the non-walker's version. | The postcard, and the reason for two Freycinet nights. **The car park fills mid-morning in January** — be at the trailhead by 08:00. Requires the parks pass (§7). |
| **Day 8 — Fri 22 Jan. Freycinet → Bay of Fires** | Drive Coles Bay → Bicheno (blowhole, penguins at dusk) → **Binalong Bay**, ~2 h net. Afternoon on the orange-lichen granite at **The Gardens** and Cosy Corner. Sleep Binalong Bay or St Helens. | The Bay of Fires is the best beach in the Capsule and it is directly **on the way** to the departure airport — the cheapest hub in the whole itinerary. |
| **Day 9 — Sat 23 Jan. Bay of Fires → Launceston** | St Helens → Launceston, 2h15–2h45 via the **Weldborough Pass** and Pyengana (St Columba Falls, the Pub in the Paddock). Afternoon: **Cataract Gorge** (free, walk from town). Dinner in Launceston; Tamar Valley cellar doors if there's time. | The winding-road day, done in daylight with a stop halfway. Launceston is small, walkable and 15 min from the gate. |
| **Day 10 — Sun 24 Jan. Depart** | Drop the car at LST, fly LST→SYD (1h47). | **Nothing booked, no drive.** The last hub is the airport's own town by design. |

**Sequencing rules this encodes**

1. **Anchor on Saturday and on MONA's closed days first**, then build outward. Salamanca is Saturday-only; MONA is shut Tue–Wed. Get those two right and the rest of the week arranges itself.
2. **Arrive into a city, depart from a city.** The buffer days at both ends must have something to do in the rain and no drive attached.
3. **Never drive more than three hours in a day**, and put the day's stops *on* the drive rather than at the end of it.
4. **Two nights minimum at any hub whose headline is a walk.** One night at Freycinet means arriving tired and leaving before the light is good.
5. **Drive in daylight.** Tasmania's roadkill rate is a national outlier; devils, quolls, wombats and wedge-tailed eagles are on the road from dusk to dawn, and Parks & Wildlife's standing advice is to slow down or not drive at all in that window. January's late sunsets (20:50 on 1 Jan, ~20:25 on 31 Jan) make this easy to obey — just don't book the 19:30 dinner two hours away.
6. **Don't hold the car on the days you don't need it.** Days 1–3 are entirely car-free (§8).

## 5. The must-dos, ranked — and the honest cut

Ranked for a **first visit, couple, no children, 9 nights**. "Cost" is per couple.

### Tier 1 — the Capsule is not the Capsule without these

| # | Thing | Time | Cost | Why it's tier 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **MONA** | Full day | **A$138** (2×A$39 + 2×A$30 ferry) | Nothing else in Australia is like it. A subterranean, deliberately antagonistic private museum reached by a camo-painted catamaran up the Derwent. It is the reason a lot of people fly to Hobart. Closed Tue–Wed; book the ferry two days ahead in summer. |
| **2** | **Wineglass Bay / Freycinet** | 2 nights | Parks pass only (walk) | The image on the postcard, and one of the rare cases where the walk to the famous view is short, well-built and worth it. Two nights, not one. |
| **3** | **Salamanca Market** | Saturday morning | **Free** | 300+ stalls in a row of 1830s sandstone warehouses. Costs nothing, takes a morning, and is the single best-value item in the Capsule — the only price is that it forces the itinerary to contain a Saturday in Hobart. |

### Tier 2 — at 9 nights you take all three; at 6 nights you take one

| # | Thing | Time | Cost | Why it's tier 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **4** | **Port Arthur + Tasman Peninsula** | 1 night | **A$110** entry (+A$360 Tasman Island Cruise, +A$70 ghost tour) | The convict story is the other half of Tasmania's identity, and the site is genuinely well interpreted. The 2-day ticket and the sea-cliff cruise turn a half-day into a proper hub. Also the site of the 1996 massacre — handled soberly on site, and worth knowing before you go. |
| **5** | **Bruny Island** | 1 day | **A$170** self-drive (or A$490 guided) | Oysters, cheese, whisky, berries, the Neck isthmus, and a genuinely dramatic south coast. The food-day of the Capsule. |
| **6** | **Bay of Fires** | 1 night | **Free** | White sand, orange-lichen granite, and empty in a way Freycinet is not. Its trump card: it sits *on the road* between Freycinet and the departure airport, so it costs one night and zero detour. |

### Tier 3 — the cut

| # | Thing | Time | Cost | Why it comes out at 9 nights |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **7** | **Cradle Mountain** | **2 nights + 2 half-days of driving** | A$105 (Annual Pass) + A$31 shuttle + ~A$700 lodging | **The honest cut.** Three reasons: (a) *transit* — 2h20 from Launceston, 4h30–5h from Hobart, and it is on the opposite corner of the island from the Bay of Fires, so the road between them runs through Launceston and any itinerary containing both has a 4½-hour driving day or an extra night to split it; (b) *weather* — January mean max around 16–17 °C, ~1,560 mm of rain a year, cloud on most days and snow recorded in every month, so a single Cradle day is a coin flip on seeing anything at all; (c) *supply* — Cradle Valley is a handful of expensive lodges that sell out for January by spring. It is not that Cradle isn't worth it. It is that Cradle is worth **three days**, and at 9 nights those three days cost you Bruny, the Tasman Peninsula and the Bay of Fires combined. |

**Also considered, also cut:** the **west coast** (Strahan, Gordon River, Queenstown — a 14-night trip), **Maria Island** (a full day plus a ferry, and it competes directly with Bruny), the **Tamar Valley** (an afternoon from Launceston if the last day runs long), **Mt Field** and the Tarkine.

**Under-rated, keep:** **kunanyi/Mt Wellington** — 30 minutes from the Hobart CBD to 1,270 m, free, and the view that makes the city legible. **Cataract Gorge** — a genuine gorge with a chairlift a 20-minute walk from Launceston's main street, free. **Bonorong Wildlife Sanctuary** (~A$42 pp, 30 min from Hobart) if seeing a Tasmanian devil matters; you will not see one in the wild.

## 6. January reality check

### Is January actually good enough? Yes — but February is quietly better.

| Factor | January reality | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Temperature** | Hobart January mean max **21.7 °C**, mean min **12.0 °C** (BOM, Ellerslie Road). Launceston runs a couple of degrees warmer by day. Cradle Mountain averages a January max around 16–17 °C. | **Positive, with layers.** Tasmania in summer is a spring day in Europe. Pack a rain shell and a warm layer for every day, including the beach days. |
| **Rain** | Hobart's January mean rainfall is about **47 mm** — Hobart is Australia's second-driest capital city, and summer is its driest stretch. The highlands are a different country: Cradle Mountain runs ~90–100 mm in January against ~1,560 mm a year. | **Strongly positive on the coast, poor in the highlands.** This asymmetry is the single best argument for the coastal arc over the lap. |
| **Daylight** | Sunset **20:50 on 1 January**, about **20:25 on 31 January**; roughly 15 hours of usable light. | **Big positive.** A 3-hour drive still leaves a full evening. It also means the wildlife hour is late — see the driving note in §4. |
| **Sea temperature** | East-coast water runs roughly **17–18 °C** in January. | **Manage expectations.** You *can* swim at Wineglass Bay and the Bay of Fires and people do. It is not a tropical swim. Nobody needs a stinger suit; some people want a wetsuit. |
| **UV** | Tasmania sits under the southern edge of the ozone hole; summer UV index routinely hits 11+ despite the mild air temperature. | **Real and under-estimated.** The mild air makes people under-dress for the sun. |
| **Weather variance** | "Four seasons in one day" is a cliché because it's accurate. Alpine and exposed-coast conditions can change inside an hour in any month. | **Plan-level, not trip-level.** It does not threaten a coastal itinerary; it does threaten a single-shot Cradle Mountain day. |
| **Bushfire** | Risk is highest **December–March**. Tasmania's precedents (2013 Dunalley, 2016 and 2019 highland fires) show roads and parks closing at short notice. | **Low probability, high impact.** Not a reason to avoid January. Is a reason to buy refundable lodging and to check TasALERT during the trip. |
| **Crowds** | Peak. **Tasmanian school holidays run right through January** — Term 1 2027 starts Thursday 4 February — and mainland NSW/Victorian holidays run to late January. The Wineglass Bay car park fills mid-morning; the Kettering–Bruny ferry queues; the Mona Roma ferry needs booking two days out. | **The real quality cost.** Manageable by starting early, not by choosing a different week. |
| **Prices** | Hobart's dearest month: **~A$156/night** average room rate against ~A$92 in June, and the country's highest occupancy at **79.3%**. | **Negative but survivable.** Budget the higher band; do not assume anything is quietly available. |
| **Car hire** | **The binding constraint.** Tasmania averages **A$98/day in January (+58% over its annual average)**, with Hobart specifically quoted at **A$171/day**. The island's fleet is small and it sells out over the Christmas–January holidays. | **Book by early October 2026.** This is the one item on the list where the failure mode is "there are no cars", not "the cars are expensive". |
| **Events** | **Taste of Summer**, Princes Wharf, Hobart — runs 27 Dec – 3 Jan annually. **Sydney–Hobart** fleet arrives from about 28–31 December (2026 race starts 13:00 on 26 Dec; a fast boat reaches Hobart in under two days, the fleet over four to five). **Cygnet Folk Festival 8–10 January 2027**, 45 min south of Hobart. **Salamanca Market** every Saturday. | **Mostly a reason to arrive after 12 January.** The 27 Dec – 3 Jan window is Hobart's hardest peak of the year on both lodging and flights. |
| **Events that no longer exist** | **MONA FOMA** — cancelled April 2024, final edition February 2024. **Falls Festival Marion Bay** — left Tasmania in 2021, last ran 2019. | **Corrects the flights research.** Neither is a January 2027 demand driver. |

### The best window inside January

**Target 13 January onward.** Before that you are paying the New Year peak — the flights doc's **A$250–400 return band for 2–12 January** rather than the A$130–220 typical band, plus Taste of Summer, the yacht-race crowd and Cygnet. From roughly the second week, fares normalise and the worst of the pressure lifts. **Tasmanian school holidays do not end until 3 February**, so the crowd relief is partial — the fare relief is not.

### Does February differ?

**Yes — and unlike the reef, here February is *better*.**

- **February is Tasmania's driest and most settled month.** Cradle Mountain's driest month is February.
- **School holidays are over from 4 February** — the crowd pressure that January cannot escape simply stops.
- **Lodging softens** from the January peak.
- **The events are better.** The **Australian Wooden Boat Festival is 5–8 February 2027** — biennial, free, 400+ wooden vessels on the Hobart waterfront, Tasmania's largest free event. Launceston's Festivale also lands in February.

**The honest recommendation: if the Plan can put Tasmania in early February and the Great Barrier Reef in January, do exactly that.** The two Capsules want opposite halves of the summer — the reef is better before February's thermal-stress and cyclone peak; Tasmania is better after January's crowds. Swapping them costs nothing and improves both.

### The other honest counter-argument: should Tasmania be in the Plan at all?

Tasmania is the most expensive block in the Plan per day, and it is the only one that *requires* a car at the year's worst rate. At 9 nights the ideal version is **€4,430**, which is **22–37% of the Plan's €12,000–20,000 budget** for a couple. Add the reef Capsule at €2,560 and the two together consume **35–58% of the entire trip budget** for 14 of its nights.

That is not an argument against Tasmania. It is an argument for deciding it deliberately: if total trip time is under about four weeks, Tasmania is the block most likely to make everything else feel rushed and everything else feel unaffordable. **Take it at 9 nights or don't take it.** A 4-night Tasmania is a bad compromise — see the floor row in §3.

## 7. Booking shortlist — attractions, passes and lodging areas

### Park passes — read this before buying

Tasmania's pass system has one trap: **the Holiday Vehicle Pass does not cover Cradle Mountain.**

| Pass | Price | Covers | Take |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Holiday Vehicle Pass, up to 2 months** | **A$98.35** per vehicle (up to 8 people) | All parks **except** Cradle Mountain — including Freycinet, Tasman NP, Mt Field, Bay of Fires reserves | **The right buy for the 9-night arc.** Nothing in the ideal itinerary needs more. |
| Daily Vehicle Pass, 24 h | A$49.15 | Same exclusion | Only worth it if you enter exactly one park, once. Two park days and the holiday pass wins. |
| **Annual Park Pass** | **A$104.75** per vehicle | **All parks, including Cradle Mountain** | **The right buy for the 12-night version.** Holiday pass + two Cradle Day Icon passes = A$159.75; the Annual Pass + two 72-hour shuttle tickets = **A$135.75**. The Annual Pass wins by ~A$24 and is simpler. *Verify the Annual Pass is vehicle-based, not per-person, at purchase.* |
| Cradle Mountain Day Icon pass | A$30.70 adult | Cradle Mountain only, **shuttle included** | The alternative if you already hold a Holiday Vehicle Pass. |
| Cradle Mountain shuttle, 72 h | A$15.50 adult | Visitor Centre ↔ Dove Lake | **Effectively compulsory October–March**: private vehicles are banned on the Dove Lake road 08:00–18:00. |

Prices last set **1 July 2026**, so they hold for January 2027 unless there is an unannounced rise. Concessions run 20% off for Australian cards — not applicable here.

### Booking order and dates

1. **Hire car — early October 2026.** Non-negotiable; this is a supply problem, not a price problem.
2. **Freycinet / Coles Bay lodging — early October 2026.** The second-tightest supply on the arc.
3. **Flights — 3–8 weeks out.** On SYD–HBA, booking ~5 weeks ahead saves roughly a third versus last-minute. Confirm the open jaw prices as expected before committing to the one-way car.
4. **Mona Roma ferry — two days ahead**, in-trip.
5. **Tasman Island Cruise, Wineglass Bay Cruise, Bruny cruise — a few weeks ahead** for January dates; these are small boats.
6. **Everything else** can be walked up.

## 9. What to re-check before booking

1. **Hire-car availability and price for the exact dates — by late September 2026.** Everything else in this Capsule is a price question; this is an availability question. Compare Hobart-pickup / Launceston-drop against Launceston-pickup / Hobart-drop, and get the one-way fee in writing.
2. **Coles Bay lodging for the exact dates — late September 2026.** Second-tightest supply.
3. **Taste of Summer 2026/27 dates.** The official site has not yet published them as of Aug 2026; secondary listings show **27 Dec 2026 – 3 Jan 2027**, consistent with the annual pattern. Only matters if the dates move earlier than planned.
4. **Whether the trip should move to early February 2027** — see §6. If it does, note the **Australian Wooden Boat Festival, 5–8 Feb 2027**, which is itself a lodging-pressure event in Hobart.
5. **Park pass prices** — set 1 July 2026; check for a 1 July 2027 revision only if the trip slips past June.
6. **That the Annual Park Pass is vehicle-based** (up to 8 people) before relying on the §7 arithmetic for the 12-night version.
7. **MONA's opening calendar over the exact dates.** Thu–Mon is the standing pattern; confirm no installation closure, and book the ferry.
8. **Open-jaw fare vs Hobart return** at booking time. The assumption that the open jaw is free rests on Aug-2026 route averages, not a live quote.
9. **Excess cover bought online, not at the counter** — A$5–12/day against A$30–45/day, and check it covers single-vehicle, unsealed-road and windscreen damage, which are the Tasmanian claims.
10. **TasALERT for bushfire and road closures**, in the week before travel and during it.

## 10. Template notes

This doc follows the structure set out in [capsule-great-barrier-reef.md §10](./capsule-great-barrier-reef.md). Two adaptations worth carrying to the next Capsule:

- **Section 2 becomes "which shape" rather than "which base"** when the Capsule is a route rather than a base. The rejected alternatives (the lap, the Hobart base, the reversed arc) still get named and priced.
- **The duration argument needs a stated binding constraint that is specific to the place.** The reef's was weather variance; Tasmania's is transit cost per hub. Stating it up front is what makes §3's table an argument rather than a preference. A new §5 ("must-dos ranked, and the honest cut") carries the item that gets removed and *why*, so the cut survives future edits.
