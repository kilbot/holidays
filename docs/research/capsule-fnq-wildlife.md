## 2. The structural question: standalone or extension?

### Verdict: extension — because the two headline species pull in opposite directions

**The binding constraint is not time or money. It is that the brief's three targets do not live in the same place, and January moves their odds in opposite directions.**

| Target | Best place | Direction from Port Douglas | January effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Saltwater croc, wild** | Daintree River | 45 min **north** — already in the Plan | **Negative.** Operator-published sighting rates fall from ~98% (Jun–Sep) to **~50/50 (Dec–Feb)**. Warm water keeps crocs submerged; they bask on banks in the cool months. |
| **Rainforest** | Daintree / Cape Tribulation | 1–2 h **north** — already in the Plan | **Positive.** Wet forest, running waterfalls, low crowds. The reef capsule already calls this "the weatherproof day". |
| **Cassowary, wild** | Etty Bay / Mission Beach | 2.5–3.5 h **south**, past Cairns | **Positive.** Fruiting season Oct–Apr; summer is when chicks are on foot with their fathers. |

Two of the three are already bought and paid for by the reef capsule's Daintree buffer day. Only the cassowary argues for a separate base — and it argues for a base **on the wrong side of Cairns**, which means burning the drive twice.

**So the extension path wins on marginal cost per unit of wildlife**, and the standalone path only becomes rational if the Plan's routing turns the drive into a Leg it was going to make anyway.

### Extension path — what it actually adds

**The single best-value line in this whole document is Hartley's on the transfer day.** It costs no Day, no extra lodging, no extra car hire, and no detour — the park is on the Captain Cook Highway between Cairns airport and Port Douglas. A$100 for the couple guarantees the crocodile and the cassowary that January might otherwise deny you, and it makes every wild sighting afterwards a bonus rather than a requirement. Do this on the arrival drive (cassowary feed 16:00) or on the departure drive (cassowary feed 09:00).

### Standalone path — Mission Beach / Etty Bay

**Etty Bay is genuinely the best wild-cassowary site in Australia.** It is a small beach 15 min off the Bruce Highway at Innisfail with a caravan park and a surf club, and a habituated cassowary population that walks onto the sand. One frequently cited traveller count is **8 sightings in 10 visits**; guide consensus calls it "almost guaranteed". Best windows are **07:00–09:00 and 16:00–18:00**. Mission Beach, 40 min further south, has the larger resident population, the Licuala fan-palm loop track (1.2 km, easy), and cheap beds.

**What it costs from Port Douglas:** Etty Bay is ~3 h each way. There is no day-trip version. You are buying **two nights and ~440 km** for one bird.

| | Out-and-back from the Port Douglas capsule | As a stop on a southbound Leg |
|---|---|---|
| Extra Days | +2 (and 2 near-useless half-days of driving) | **+1 to +2, with the driving already paid for** |
| Extra cost/couple | ≈ **A$620 / €380** | ≈ **A$360 / €220** |
| Verdict | **No.** | **Yes — take it.** |

**The decision rule:** if the Plan leaves Far North Queensland by flying out of Cairns, Mission Beach is a dead-end detour and the answer is no. If the Plan drives or buses south down the Bruce Highway (Townsville, Magnetic Island, Airlie Beach, Whitsundays), then **Mission Beach is directly on the route** and two nights there is the cheapest wildlife in the whole trip: Etty Bay, the Licuala track and the South Mission Beach roads are all free.

### Townsville / Billabong Sanctuary — ruled out

Billabong Sanctuary (Nome, 17 km south of Townsville) is a good park — salties, Southern cassowaries, koalas, **A$54 adult**, 09:00–16:00, croc hand-feed A$100. But it is a **wildlife park**, i.e. the same product Hartley's sells for A$50 twenty-five minutes from Port Douglas, ~350 km / 4¼ h further south. **There is no reason to drive to Townsville for animals.** Keep it as a line item only if the Plan already routes through Townsville for Magnetic Island, in which case it is a fine half-day.

## 3. How long — min, ideal, max

The binding constraint is **dawn and dusk**. Every wildlife target in this capsule — cassowary, croc, most birds — is a crepuscular animal, and every one of them is *unavailable* to a couple sleeping in Port Douglas, because the ferry is 45 min away and Cape Tribulation is 1 h 45 beyond that. The reef, by contrast, is a mid-morning product. **They do not compete for hours. They compete for where you sleep.**

| | Extra days | What you get | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bolt-on (floor)** | **+0** | A 1 h croc cruise at a convenient (i.e. wrong) time of day, plus a wildlife park. Rainforest already in the Plan. | **Works, and is genuinely defensible.** With Hartley's as the backstop you are guaranteed both animals. What you don't get is *wild* — and in January the wild croc is a coin flip anyway. |
| **Ideal** | **+1 day / +1 night at Cow Bay or Cape Trib** | A **dusk** drive and a **dawn** boardwalk north of the ferry; the cruise re-timed to a **low tide**; the option of the 2 h dawn/dusk cruise. | **The pick.** One cheap night converts the whole block from "we did a croc cruise" to "we spent a night in the rainforest and went looking". |
| **Maximum** | **+2** (Mission Beach as a Leg stop) | Etty Bay at dawn and dusk on two consecutive days — the highest wild-cassowary odds available anywhere. | Only with a southbound Leg. Otherwise the marginal day buys driving. |

**Why not more.** Beyond three wildlife days in this region you are re-walking the same boardwalks. The Atherton Tablelands add platypus and tree-kangaroos but are a different capsule with a different base, and cassowary odds there are low.

| **Mission Beach** — Licuala fan-palm track, South Mission Beach roads, the transfer station, Licuala Lodge | **High over a morning**; largest accessible resident population in Australia | 2 h S of Cairns, 40 min S of Etty Bay | The base if you're staying. Needs a night; a drive-through is much weaker. |
| **Daintree / Cape Tribulation** — Cape Trib Road at dawn/dusk, Dubuji / Marrdja / Jindalba boardwalks | **Moderate** — real sightings, regularly reported, but not likely on a single daytime pass | **Already in the Plan** | The only *free* shot. Maximised by the extra night; near-zero without it. |
| **Atherton Tablelands** | **Low** — dense forest, dispersed birds | 1.5 h SW of Cairns | Not worth routing for. |

**Timing beats location.** Sunrise to two hours after, and the last two hours before dusk, at any of these sites, beats midday at the best of them.

**January is on your side for this one.** Fruiting season runs Oct–Apr; summer is when chicks are following their fathers and foraging near forest edges. This is the one part of the brief the wet season improves.

**Safety, not decoration.** Cassowaries are large, wild, and capable of serious injury, especially with chicks. Keep distance, never feed, never follow, and put something solid between you and the bird if it approaches.

## 6. January reality check

### Crocodiles: the honest correction

The premise that "croc activity is HIGH in the wet" is **half right and misleading for a tourist**. Crocodiles *are* more active and more widely distributed in the wet — the water is high, they move, it is breeding and nesting season. But **activity is not visibility**. In the cool dry months crocs haul out onto exposed mud banks to thermoregulate, which is precisely what makes them easy to see from a boat. In January the water is warm, they have no reason to bask, and they stay submerged.

Both long-running Daintree operators publish the same shape of number:

- **~98% sighting rate June–September**; approximately **50/50 December–February**, coinciding with breeding and wet season (Bruce Belcher's / Daintree River Cruises FAQ).
- *"The most difficult time is in the Summer on a high tide but we can usually find a small one at low tide"* — Solar Whisper FAQ. Note "a small one": the January failure mode is not seeing nothing, it is seeing a 1.5 m juvenile instead of a 4 m adult.

**Mitigations, in order of leverage:**

1. **Book a low tide.** Both operators say low tide exposes bank and reduces water volume. This is free and it is the single biggest lever.
2. **Book dawn or dusk.** Solar Whisper's 2 h cruise (A$80 pp, max 10 passengers, by arrangement) exists for exactly this.
3. **Use the free-return guarantee.** Both Solar Whisper and Belcher's offer a free repeat cruise if no crocodile is seen — worth real money only if you have a second day in the area, which is another argument for the extra night.
4. **Take a silent boat.** Solar Whisper is solar-electric and can approach closer without engine noise. This matters most when the animals are shy.
5. **Accept the park.** Hartley's exists for this.

**Rain does not stop a cruise.** The boats are roofed, the rain is warm, and operators report wildlife viewing is often good in light rain. Only genuine river flooding stops them.

**Crocodile danger is genuinely higher in the wet.** High, turbid water means crocodiles move into places they aren't in the dry. Do not swim or stand at the edge of any waterway from the Daintree River mouth to the lower Mossman. Mossman Gorge's swimming hole is above the croc line but carries a **flash-flood** risk in January instead — do not enter if the water is rising or discoloured.

### The honest counter-argument

*"If January is the worst croc-viewing month and the Daintree cassowary odds are only moderate, why not just do Hartley's and skip the wild version?"*

That is a defensible position, and it is exactly what the **+0 day bolt-on** buys. The counter is that a croc cruise costs A$70 for the couple against A$100 for the park, the boat is an hour on a beautiful river with birds and snakes and tree frogs regardless of whether a crocodile turns up, and the free-return guarantee means a miss on Day 3 can be re-rolled on Day 4. **Do both.** The wild attempt is cheap; the park is the insurance; neither costs a Day.

### Kuranda: Scenic Railway + Skyrail — worth it?

**Verdict: tourist trap for this couple, with one January-specific exception.**

| Option | Adult | Per couple |
|---|---|---|
| Skyrail + Kuranda Scenic Railway combo (either direction, self-drive or with transfers) | **A$145.50** | **A$291** |
| Skyrail return only | **A$111** | **A$222** |
| Gold Class rail upgrade | A$57 one way | — |

Prices valid 1 Apr 2026 – 31 Mar 2027.

**Why skip it.** A$291 buys roughly two hours of transport over rainforest this couple will already have *walked through* at Mossman Gorge and Cape Tribulation, terminating in a village of souvenir shops. The Kuranda wildlife attractions (Birdworld, Koala Gardens, Butterfly Sanctuary) are three separate paid entries that duplicate what Hartley's delivers in one A$50 ticket. It is also a Cairns-based day — a 2 h round trip from Port Douglas before you start.

**The one honest argument for it.** **Barron Falls is only spectacular in the wet**, and January is when it runs hardest. Both the train and Skyrail stop at Barron Falls lookouts. If a reef day is cancelled and the sky is grey, this is a genuinely good rain-day purchase — and if you buy it, buy **Skyrail return (A$222)** rather than the combo: the cableway has the better falls viewing and the Rainforest Discovery Zone, and the train's appeal is heritage rather than scenery. Note the combo page shows a **Wednesday–Sunday** operating pattern for the 2026-27 season — confirm against your dates.

## 9. What to re-check before booking

1. **Daintree tide table for the chosen date.** The highest-leverage decision in this capsule: book the croc cruise to a **low tide**, not to a convenient departure time.
