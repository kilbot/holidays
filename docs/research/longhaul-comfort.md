# Long-haul comfort: Europe → Perth (Dec 2026), east coast → Valencia (Jan–Feb 2027)

Research for [issue #3](https://github.com/kilbot/holidays/issues/3). Researched 2026-08-26.

**Question.** What is the most comfortable way to fly *economy* from Valencia to Perth in
mid-December 2026, and back from Sydney/Melbourne/Brisbane to Valencia in late January–mid
February 2027, at the best price for that comfort?

## Bottom line

**Fly Singapore Airlines from Barcelona, break the trip with a night in Singapore, and book it
now.** Train Valencia→Barcelona the day before; SQ387 BCN 12:35 → SIN 08:25+1 on the A350-900
(3-3-3, ~18" wide — a full inch more than the 787s and 10-abreast 777s that dominate this route);
overnight at Changi; 5-hour hop to Perth. **Singapore is the same time zone as Perth**, so the
hotel night absorbs the whole 7-hour eastward shift and they land ready for Christmas.
**€1,500–2,300 per person**, plus hotels.

Three findings drive that, and each overturns a reasonable prior:

1. **The Gulf is a reliability problem in December 2026, not a comfort choice.** The 2026
   Iran-crisis airspace closures are still not resolved; Emirates and Qatar are at ~75–85% of
   pre-crisis schedules and Gulf airspace remains restricted. Against a hard Christmas-in-Perth
   anchor, that outweighs the Emirates A380's excellent seat.
2. **Singapore Airlines starts flying to Barcelona and Madrid on 26 October 2026** — six weeks
   before they travel, and reachable by train. That route did not exist a year ago.
3. **The A380 upper-deck instinct is right, but almost nothing delivers it.** Singapore and
   Qantas have *no* economy upstairs; Emirates is deleting its last upper-deck economy cabin;
   British Airways is retrofitting its away. No A380 with upper-deck economy flies any
   Europe↔Australia sector this winter.

**How to read this.** Claims are tagged:

- **[VERIFIED]** — airline schedule, press release, seat map, or dated trade reporting.
- **[LORE]** — established forum/reviewer consensus (FlyerTalk, r/flights, Australian Frequent
  Flyer, Head for Points, Mainly Miles, aeroLOPA/SeatGuru-type seat data). Usually right,
  occasionally stale.
- **[CHECK AT BOOKING]** — true today, but volatile enough that it must be re-confirmed.

## 1. The thing that changed everything: the 2026 Gulf airspace disruption

This dominates every recommendation below, and it is the single most important finding.

**[VERIFIED]** The 2026 Israel–US–Iran conflict closed commercial airspace over Iran and much of
the Gulf. At the peak, Qatar Airways, Emirates and Etihad were "almost entirely grounded", and
roughly 52,000 flights were cancelled. The traditional Kangaroo Route through the Gulf broke.

**[VERIFIED]** As of mid-August 2026 it is *not* fixed:

- EASA's conflict-zone guidance for the Persian Gulf and Gulf of Oman has been extended to at
  least **31 August 2026**, advising operators not to fly at *any* flight level over Bahrain,
  Kuwait, Qatar or the UAE.
- Doha airspace is **partially closed**; arrivals, departures and transits are confined to
  specific published corridors. Dubai is **restricted** to limited approved corridors with
  expected delays.
- Recovery is partial: Qatar Airways has restored ~85% of its pre-crisis schedule, Emirates
  ~75%, Etihad is on "phased resumption" to ~80 destinations.
- Several non-Gulf carriers (Cathay Pacific, Lufthansa, Singapore Airlines) postponed their own
  Middle East resumptions to **late October 2026**.

**[VERIFIED]** Knock-on effects that matter directly to this trip:

- **Qantas Perth–London lost its nonstop westbound.** QF9 has run via Singapore since 4 March
  2026 (as QF209 since 16 June). The Singapore refuelling stop has been extended through
  **31 January 2027**, with nonstop not expected back until **February 2027**. The longer
  Central Asia routing pushes the 787-9 past its practical range with a full payload.
- **The eastbound QF10 London→Perth remains nonstop** and is scheduled nonstop from 25 October
  2026 onward — i.e. it covers the December 2026 outbound. **[CHECK AT BOOKING]** The route is
  asymmetric right now and that asymmetry has already moved twice.
- Flights between Southeast Asia and Europe now overfly Central Asia, the Caucasus and Türkiye
  to avoid both Iranian and Russian restricted airspace, adding block time.

**[VERIFIED]** Gulf carriers *are* selling December 2026 Australia flights, and in volume —
Emirates is the largest Australia–Middle East operator this December (619 flights / 283,479
seats), with Qatar second. Qatar has confirmed daily DOH–PER (777-300ER) and Virgin Australia
resumes Perth–Doha on 9 December 2026.

**[LORE / judgement]** But Australian Frequent Flyer's read is that Gulf fares stay competitive
precisely *because* you carry the risk: short-notice cancellations are live, and Australian
government travel advisories can leave travel insurance not responding to a Gulf-transit
disruption. Their summary is that you "might get stuck in transit".

> **Implication for this trip.** There is a hard anchor — Christmas in Perth with family. A
> routing that is 5% more comfortable but carries a materially higher chance of stranding the
> couple in a restricted-airspace hub on 22 December is *not* the comfort-optimal routing.
> **For December 2026, Asian hubs (Singapore, Hong Kong, Kuala Lumpur) beat Gulf hubs on
> comfort-per-euro once reliability is priced in.** This conveniently agrees with the stated
> preference for Changi.

## 2. What actually determines economy comfort: seat geometry

The single most useful frame. Marketing talks about pitch; over 13+ hours, **width and whether
you get a private pair** matter more.

**[VERIFIED / LORE]** Cabin geometry by type and layout:

| Aircraft & layout | Abreast | Seat width | Typical pitch | Couple gets a pair with no stranger? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A380 upper deck, 2-4-2** | 8 | ~18" | 31–32" | **Yes** — A/B and J/K side pairs |
| **A380 main deck, 3-4-3** | 10 | 17.5–18" | 32–34" (Emirates) | Only in the rear taper rows |
| **A350, 3-3-3** | 9 | ~18" | 31–32" | No |
| **A330, 2-4-2** | 8 | ~18" | 31–32" | **Yes** |
| **787, 3-3-3** | 9 | **17.2"** | 31–32" | No |
| **777, 3-4-3** | 10 | **17.0–17.2"** | 31–32" | No |
| **777, 3-3-3** (rare) | 9 | ~18.5" | 34" | No |

Three consequences:

1. **The A380 and A350 are the comfortable widebodies in economy; the 787 and the 10-abreast 777
   are not.** The A380 is wide enough that even 10-abreast gives ~18" seats. The A350 at
   9-abreast gives ~18". The 787 at 9-abreast and the 777 at 10-abreast both land at ~17.2". That
   ~0.8" per seat is the whole difference between the "good" and "bad" long-haul economy cabins.
   **The instinct that "A380s are comfortable" is correct, and the mechanism is fuselage width.**
2. **The upper-deck instinct is also correct, and for a better reason than expected.** On a 2-4-2
   upper deck, a couple books A/B or J/K and has *no third passenger in the row*. On any 3-3-3 or
   3-4-3 cabin, a couple always has a stranger. **[LORE]** Head for Points and the seat-map
   community also credit the A380 upper deck with a quieter cabin (further from engines and
   hydraulics), a much smaller cabin (~104 seats vs ~199 below), and extra personal storage bins
   under the windows — a feature unique to the A380 upper deck.
3. **Main-deck A380 has a consolation prize.** **[LORE]** The A380 main deck tapers at the rear,
   so the last few rows drop from 3 seats to 2 on the sides. On Emirates, rows 67 and 80 (B/C and
   H/J) are the widely-cited couple seats; rows 68 and 81 A/K get extra legroom. The same taper
   trick works on most 777 and A380 rear cabins.

**[LORE]** Independent ranking corroboration: AirlineRatings' 2026 long-haul assessment puts
**Singapore Airlines 1st (36.95), Qatar Airways 2nd (36.24), Cathay Pacific 3rd (33.41)**, and
Japan Airlines has taken "best economy seat" for the sixth year running (JAL runs 787s at
2-4-2 / 8-abreast — the exception that proves the width rule, but JAL does not serve Perth).

### The A380s that *are* on this trip's likely path (without upper-deck economy)

**[VERIFIED]**

- **Qantas SYD–SIN–LHR (QF1/QF2) daily A380.** SYD–SIN A380 rises **7 → 13 weekly on 7 December

  departures ex-Singapore. Very relevant to the **return**.
- **Singapore Airlines winter 2026/27: SIN–SYD 2x daily A380, SIN–MEL daily A380 (restored,

- **Emirates A380** serves 21 European airports including Madrid and Barcelona — though **Barcelona

### Emirates: the one place the "narrow 777" reputation is measured, not vibes

**[VERIFIED]** Emirates A380 economy is **18.0" wide at 32" pitch**; the Emirates 777-300ER is
**17.0" at 32" pitch**. A full inch of width, same pitch, and the A380's wider fuselage makes the
identical 3-4-3 feel less boxed in.

**[VERIFIED] But DXB–PER has flip-flopped.** Emirates suspended the A380 to Perth after 14 March
2026 in favour of a 354-seat 777-300ER, with an A380 return planned for 1 July 2026, and has since
cut Perth from twice-daily to daily. **[CHECK AT BOOKING]** — do not assume an A380 on DXB–PER.

### Best seats, if an A380 is flown

**[VERIFIED / LORE]**

- **BA A380 upper deck:** window seats A/K in rows 70–83; **70A/70K and 80A/80K** are the picks
  (front row, extra legroom). Windows have side bins — **except exit rows, which have no windows
  at all**. Main-deck fallback: **25D** (no seat in front).
- **Lufthansa A380 upper deck:** **95E, 96A, 96C** are cited as the quietest economy on the
  aircraft; rows 96–98 A/K have the side storage. Avoid 95A/95K (no window).
- **Qatar A380 upper deck:** row 25 (extra legroom + locker, but near the noisy lounge), row 30
  (extra legroom, no locker). The UD advantage is entirely in the window pairs — the middle block
  is no better than the main deck.
- **Emirates A380 main deck:** **68A/68K and 81A/81K** — no seat in front, big legroom, storage.
  Quietest section is rows 80–88 at the rear, then 43–51 at the front.

**[LORE]** The upper-deck mini-cabin advantage — quieter, less galley and lavatory traffic, fewer
people walking past, side bins that double as a shelf — is consistently attested on FlyerTalk's
long-running BA A380 seating thread and in the enthusiast press, but is opinion rather than
measurement. **The one genuinely structural advantage is that 2-4-2 gives a window passenger one
neighbour instead of two — and a couple, zero strangers.**

## 5. Origin arbitrage: Valencia vs Barcelona / Madrid / London / Milan

**The headline finding: Singapore Airlines returns to Spain in exactly this travel window.**

**[VERIFIED]** From **26 October 2026**, Singapore Airlines launches **SIN–BCN–MAD**, its first
Madrid service in 22 years and its 15th European destination, on the **A350-900 Long Haul**
(253 seats: 42 Business / 24 Premium Economy / **187 Economy**, 3-3-3 at ~32" pitch and ~18"
width). Reported as five-times-weekly by SIA's own announcement and by trade press; one
frequent-flyer outlet reports it as daily — **[CHECK AT BOOKING]**.

Published inaugural timings:

| | Routing | Times |
|---|---|---|
| **Westbound SQ388** | SIN → BCN → MAD | SIN 23:30 → BCN 06:40+1 → BCN 07:40 → MAD 08:50 |
| **Eastbound SQ387** | MAD → BCN → SIN | MAD 10:00 → BCN 11:15 → BCN 12:35 → **SIN 08:25+1** |

Two things fall out of this:

1. **Barcelona is the better Spanish origin than Madrid**, even though Madrid is the closer
   train. Boarding at **BCN gives a nonstop BCN→SIN sector**; boarding at MAD adds a
   through-stop at Barcelona. Valencia→Barcelona is ~3h by AVE/Euromed vs ~1h45
   Valencia→Madrid — **[LORE]** worth the extra 75 minutes of train to delete a stop from a
   24-hour journey.
2. **The eastbound arrival into Changi at 08:25 is close to ideal.** It sets up either a
   comfortable same-day afternoon connection to Perth (arriving that night), or a clean
   overnight-stopover break with a full day in Singapore. Note SQ387 only reaches BCN at 12:35
   departure, so this is a *daytime* departure from Spain and an early-morning arrival in
   Singapore — good for resetting to Perth time.

**[VERIFIED]** Singapore Airlines' broader winter 2026 Europe schedule is its largest ever:
**128 weekly departures to 15 cities** — London (Heathrow 4x daily + Gatwick daily, up to 6
daily combined), Paris CDG 11x weekly, Frankfurt 14x, Zurich 7x, Milan going **daily from 25
October 2026**, Munich new 3x weekly from 26 October, plus Barcelona, Madrid, Brussels,
Amsterdam, Istanbul, Copenhagen, Rome and Manchester (daily from October). The 3x weekly
SIN–MXP–BCN service (SQ377/378) is **cancelled from 27 October 2026**, replaced by the Madrid
tag and the daily Milan nonstop.

**And Madrid gains a second one.** **[VERIFIED]** Cathay Pacific goes **daily HKG–Madrid from 25
October 2026** (up from 4x weekly), driven by new Iberia codeshares. Critically, **Cathay's
Barcelona service is summer-seasonal and does not operate in December** — so for this trip
Cathay means Madrid, not Barcelona. **[VERIFIED]** Cathay also runs **twice-daily HKG–PER** from
late October. And **China Southern has launched its first-ever Spain route, MAD–Guangzhou**,
connecting to CAN–PER.

### Through-ticket vs self-connect — the lore is unanimous

**[LORE, very high consensus]** One Mile at a Time's rule for separate tickets: **at least four
hours, and more when checking bags**, with a later flight that would still get you there in time —
and he accepts short buffers only on the *homeward* leg. General guidance is 3–4h minimum with
checked bags, 4–5h across terminals; the named failure modes are bag-drop cutoffs 45–60 min before
an international departure and 30+ minute carousel delays. FlyerTalk on Madrid specifically: *"If
you are planning on separate tickets, the risk is too high. If the first flight is late, the
second airline may treat you as a no-show."*

This matters more than usual here: **a missed connection on a sold-out mid-December Perth flight
is close to uncoverable** — the next available *pair* of seats may be days away at walk-up prices,
and the downside isn't €200, it's Christmas in Perth. Preference order:

| | Structure | Protection | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Best** | **Train VLC→MAD/BCN the day before, sleep there**, then one through-ticket ex-MAD/BCN | Full | A €80–140 hotel night eliminates connection risk on the highest-stakes travel day and starts a 24h journey rested. **This is the answer.** |
| **2. Good** | One ticket **VLC→PER** with the feeder flown as part of the fare (Iberia/Vueling to MAD/BCN, or via CDG/AMS/FRA/IST) | Full — airline must rebook | The only rail-free option with real protection. Confirm it's a single PNR. |
| **3. OK** | Train same day, arriving **5h+** before departure | None, but rail is highly redundant (25 trains/day) | A missed train costs 40 min, not a day. |
| **4. Avoid** | Separate positioning **flight** same day | None | A cancelled Ryanair rotation in sold-out December has no recovery. |
| **5. Avoid** | Self-connect onto an LCC at SIN/KUL | None; separate bags | Incompatible with comfort-per-euro. |

### Milan, Rome, Paris, Frankfurt, London

**[VERIFIED]** Milan's SQ connection **disappears on 27 October 2026** (SIN–MXP–BCN discontinued),
which removes the main reason to position to Italy. Paris and Frankfurt offer nothing Madrid lacks
for Perth. **London is the one real alternative** — it is the only city with a nonstop to Perth,
plus Malaysia (KUL), Cathay, Singapore, Qatar and Emirates. Against it: **UK Air Passenger Duty on
ultra-long-haul economy is £106 per person** from 1 April 2026 (rising again in April 2027) —
about **€245 of extra tax for the couple** that a Spanish or Italian origin simply doesn't pay.

**Verdict: the meaningful arbitrage is routing quality, not price.** Madrid unlocks Cathay + China
Southern + Qatar/Emirates. Barcelona unlocks the Singapore Airlines nonstop. London unlocks the
Qantas nonstop and the A380s, at a €245 tax premium and an unprotected positioning leg.

## 6. Stopover strategy

### The argument that settles where the stopover goes

**[VERIFIED]** Europe → Australia is **eastward** (Madrid UTC+1 in December → Perth UTC+8 = **+7
hours**). Australia → Europe is **westward** (Sydney UTC+11 in February → Madrid UTC+1 = **−10
hours**). Several travel sites get this backwards by calling the *return* "eastward" — it isn't.

**Eastward is the hard direction.** About 75% of people have a circadian period slightly longer
than 24 hours, so delaying the clock (westward) is natural and advancing it (eastward) fights
biology. A 6-hour westward shift resolves in ~6 days; the same shift eastward takes **8–10 days**.

Now the decisive geographical fact:

> **Singapore is in the same time zone as Perth. Both are UTC+8.**

So an **overnight in Singapore puts them on Perth time before the final 5-hour hop.** They sleep
one real night already adjusted, then arrive in Perth essentially on local time with the whole
7-hour shift absorbed by a hotel bed rather than by a seat. Compare Doha at UTC+3 — only **2 of
the 7 hours** absorbed, then an 11-hour flight with 5 hours of shift still to do.

Leg balance says the same thing. Doha splits Europe–Perth roughly **7h + 11h** — lopsided with
the *hard* leg last. Singapore splits it **~13h + 5h** — lopsided with the *easy* leg last, and
the long leg flown while still on home time.

> **Put the substantial stopover outbound, in Singapore.** Eastward deserves the buffer; SIN is
> Perth time so the night does the adjustment work; and they are flying into a Christmas
> anchor — arriving functional on 15 December is worth more than arriving functional in February.
> On the westward return, a short transit is fine.

**[LORE]** One refinement from the sleep literature: on the westward return, an **afternoon**
arrival into Europe beats an early-morning one — morning light after a westward flight pushes the
clock the wrong way. SQ388's 06:40 arrival into Barcelona is the one weak point of the SQ return.

### Does a stopover cost extra?

**[LORE, consistent across sources]** On a through ticket, a stopover at the carrier's own hub is
usually free or near-free — the fare is built on origin–destination, not elapsed time — and
occasionally prices *lower* than a short connection. Cathay is the notable exception with an
explicit stopover fee. **The mechanics matter: book it as a multi-city itinerary before ticketing**,
never as two separate tickets, or they lose baggage through-checking and misconnect protection —
exactly what an overnight stopover most needs.

**The clean way to test it for their actual dates:** price (A) Europe→PER with a 2-hour connection
and (B) the identical dates as a multi-city with a 2-night hub break. The delta is the answer.

### Visas and bags

**[VERIFIED]**

- **Singapore** — visa-free 90 days for Spanish and Australian passports. The **SG Arrival Card is
  mandatory and free**, submitted **within 3 days of arrival** at `eservices.ica.gov.sg` or the
  MyICA app. **Exemption: passengers transiting without clearing immigration don't need it** — so
  staying airside needs no SGAC, but a hotel night or the Free Singapore Tour does. ICA warns
  against the many commercial lookalike sites; **use only the official portal.**
- **Qatar** — visa-free for both (Schengen 90 days per 180; Australian 30 days, extendable).
  Requires passport valid ≥3 months and **a confirmed hotel booking on arrival** — which the
  Discover Qatar package satisfies automatically.
- **UAE** — Spanish/Schengen 90 days visa-free. **[CHECK]** Australian stay length is reported
  inconsistently (30 vs 90 days). Passport needs 6 months' validity and must be machine-readable.
- **Hong Kong** — visa-free 90 days for both. **Pre-Arrival Registration does not apply** to
  Spanish or Australian passports.
- **Malaysia** — MDAC required if entering, submitted ≤3 days before arrival; **exempt if you stay
  airside**. Only the official `imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac/main` portal — every other MDAC
  site is a paid third party.

**[LORE] Baggage.** Under ~24 hours on a single ticket, bags are normally **through-checked** to
the final destination and you go to the hotel with hand baggage. **Over 24 hours counts as a
stopover, not a connection** — bags are typically *not* tagged through, so you claim them, clear
customs and re-check next day. Some carriers cut over earlier (United at 12h, American at 16h).
**Practical rule: pack an overnight bag in carry-on regardless, and ask explicitly at origin
check-in — "is this tagged to PER or to SIN?"** None of SIN/DOH/DXB/HKG/KUL forces a customs
re-check on a pure airside transit.

## 8. Shortlist: strategies ranked by comfort per euro

Ranked with reliability priced in — a routing that is marginally more comfortable but carries a
materially higher chance of stranding them on 22 December is not comfort-optimal against a hard
Christmas anchor.

### 1. **The Changi Line** — Barcelona → Singapore → Perth on Singapore Airlines ★ recommended

**Routing.** Train Valencia → Barcelona (~3h) **the day before**, sleep in Barcelona. **SQ387
BCN 12:35 → SIN 08:25+1.** Overnight (or a long day) in Singapore. **SIN → PER**, 5h, arriving
Perth adjusted. Return: **SYD/MEL → SIN on the Singapore A380**, then **SQ388 SIN 23:30 → BCN
06:40**, train home.

**Aircraft and seat.** **A350-900 Long Haul** on the 13h sector — 253 seats (42J / 24W / **187Y**),
economy 3-3-3 at **~18" wide, 32" pitch**. SIN–PER is A350-900 or 787-10 (**request the A350
rotation**; at 5h it's a tiebreak, not a dealbreaker). The **return gets an A380** on SYD/MEL–SIN:
SIN–SYD is 2x daily A380 and SIN–MEL daily A380 through late March 2027 — main deck 3-4-3 at ~18",
so aim for the **rear taper rows** where the sides drop to two seats.

**Stopover plan.** One or two nights in Singapore, **outbound**. This is the whole point: Singapore
is **the same time zone as Perth**, so the hotel night does the 7-hour eastward adjustment and the
final leg is a short hop into a family Christmas. Free Singapore Tour if the connection is 5.5–24h;
economy gets ~SGD 20 in Changi vouchers on a 24h+ revenue stop. Hotel at market rate, **€190–290/
night** — this stopover is not subsidised, and the SIA holiday package explicitly excludes
Christmas.

**Price band.** **€1,500–2,300 pp**, plus ~€200–500 for two Singapore hotel nights.

**Booking urgency.** High, and specifically: the route is **brand new (launches 26 October 2026)**,
Barcelona drops from 7 to **5 weekly** departures under the new pattern, and December is peak.
New-route inventory on a peak date does not linger.

**Why it wins.** #1-ranked long-haul airline for 2026; 18" seats on the long sector; Changi, which
is both their stated preference and the consensus best layover airport; a 3h train rather than a
positioning flight; **avoids Gulf airspace entirely**; and the time-zone trick makes the stopover
do real work rather than just breaking up the trip.

**Watch for.** SQ387's **06:40 arrival into Barcelona** on the return is the one flaw — early
morning light after a westward flight pushes the clock the wrong way.

### 2. **The Value-Comfort Line** — Madrid → Hong Kong → Perth on Cathay Pacific

**Routing.** Train Valencia → Madrid (**1h56**, ~25 trains/day), sleep in Madrid. **MAD → HKG
daily from 25 October 2026** (up from 4x weekly). **HKG → PER twice daily**, 7h40. Return
east coast → HKG → MAD.

**Aircraft and seat.** **A350 on both sectors** — 3-3-3 at **~18" wide, 32" pitch**. HKG–PER is the
A350-900 in 280 seats (38J / 28W / 214Y). Cathay ranks **3rd** for long-haul in 2026.

**Stopover plan.** Hong Kong overnight. **[VERIFIED]** Cathay charges a stopover fee by default and
the current waiver promo does **not** cover Europe–Australia — price it both ways. Hotels are the
cheapest of any hub here: Tung Chung airport-area **€95–150/night**, and **January is Hong Kong's
cheapest month**. Free HKIA guided tour for 7h+ layovers. HKG is UTC+8, so the same time-zone trick
as Singapore applies.

**Price band.** **€1,300–1,900 pp.** Hong Kong is the cheapest hub in the aggregator data
(€1,007 average return ex-Barcelona).

**Booking urgency.** High. Same December peak curve.

**Why it ranks here.** Nearly the same hardware as strategy 1 for €200–400 less per person, on a
shorter train, with much cheaper hotels — the best raw comfort-per-euro ratio on the list. It
loses to strategy 1 only on airport quality and the Perth time-zone alignment being equal but the
final leg longer (7h40 vs 5h).

**Watch for.** **Cathay's Barcelona service is summer-seasonal and does not operate in December** —
this strategy is Madrid, not Barcelona.

### 3. **The Upper-Deck Play** — London → Singapore on the British Airways A380

**Routing.** **VLC → LHR on BA** (through-ticketed, oneworld), then **LHR → SIN on the BA A380,
daily from 7 September 2026 through at least 17 March 2027**, then **SIN → PER on Qantas**.

**Aircraft and seat.** The only routing on this list that gives the couple **a row to themselves**.
BA's A380 upper deck is **World Traveller in 2-4-2, rows 70–83, 104 seats, 17.6" wide, 31" pitch**.
Book **70A/70K or 80A/80K** — front row, extra legroom, side storage bins. Avoid exit rows, which
have **no windows at all**. **[LORE]** The upper deck is quieter, has far less galley and lavatory
traffic, and is a third the size of the cabin below.

**Stopover plan.** Same Singapore overnight as strategy 1, with the same time-zone benefit.

**Price band.** **€1,600–2,400 pp**, **plus £106 per person of UK Air Passenger Duty** (~€245 for
the couple) that a Spanish origin simply does not pay.

**Booking urgency.** High — and there is a **hard verification gate** (below) that may kill it.

**Why it's third despite being the "dream" seat.** ⚠️ **Retrofit roulette.** BA's A380 refit began
June 2026 and the new 421-seat layout puts **110 Club Suites across the entire upper deck, moving
all economy to the main deck**. The first refurbished aircraft is due back in service **late 2026**
and the fleet completes end-2027. A December booking is a coin-flip on the specific airframe. BA's
31" pitch and weaker soft product also cost it against Singapore Airlines.

**Only book this if the seat map at the moment of booking shows the 2-4-2 upper deck.** If it
doesn't, this collapses to a worse version of strategy 1.

**Reliable variant.** **VLC → Munich → Bangkok on Lufthansa's A380** (35 upper-deck economy seats,
rows 95–99, 2-4-2, **18" wide**) then **BKK → PER on Thai** — all Star Alliance, one ticket.
Lufthansa's 2026 VantageXL retrofit **touches Business only**, so the upper-deck economy seats
**survive**, making this the *safest* upper-deck-economy bet available. It costs a third sector and
swaps Changi for Bangkok. Pick **95E, 96A or 96C**; avoid 95A/95K (no window).

### 4. **The Budget Floor** — Madrid → Guangzhou → Perth on China Southern

**Routing.** Train Valencia → Madrid, then **MAD → CAN → PER** — China Southern's first-ever Spain
route, connecting to its Guangzhou–Perth service.

**Aircraft and seat.** Modern widebody hardware, weaker service, and Guangzhou is a mediocre
layover by every ranking here.

**Stopover plan.** Straight through, or a short CAN transit. Not a stopover you'd choose for
pleasure.

**Price band.** **€900–1,300 pp** — China Southern averages **€868** return MAD–PER in the
aggregator data.

**Booking urgency.** High.

**Why it's on the list.** It is the **cheapest genuine single-connection through-ticket** with real
interline protection, and it avoids Gulf airspace. Against a €6,000–10,000 per-person budget, it
frees **€400–800 per person** for east-coast capsules — which may be better comfort-per-euro spent
on the ground than in the air. Include it as the honest floor, not as a recommendation.

**What it is not.** **Do not confuse this with Scoot / AirAsia X / Batik.** Those have **no European
routes at all** and can only appear as the second half of an unprotected two-ticket self-connect.
Treat any sub-€900 quote as a two-stop self-connect, not a comfort option.

### 5. **The Famous Nonstop** — London → Perth on Qantas QF10 — *considered and rejected*

**Routing.** Position to London, then **QF10 LHR → PER nonstop, 16h40**, filed nonstop from
25 October 2026.

**Why it's rejected.** This is the option everyone reaches for first, and it is the **worst seat on
the longest sit**: the Qantas 787-9 is **3-3-3 at 17.2"** — the narrowest geometry in this entire
document — for nearly 17 unbroken hours. **[LORE]** Reviews split hard, with the negative ones
reporting cramping after several hours and tight aisles. It also costs **£106 pp of APD**, prices at
or above the top of the premium band (**€1,800–2,600+ pp**), and needs an unprotected positioning
leg into a UK hub in December.

**Worth knowing anyway:** the route is **asymmetric right now**. Westbound PER → LHR has run **via
Singapore since March 2026** and stays that way **through 31 January 2027**; only the eastbound
direction they'd actually fly is nonstop. If the goal is fewest segments, this wins. If the goal is
comfort per euro, it loses to every other strategy on this list.

### Cross-cutting moves that apply to all five

1. **Book it as a multi-city open-jaw** (BCN/MAD → PER … SYD/MEL/BNE → BCN/MAD), never as two
   one-ways. Evidence says an open-jaw prices **at or slightly below** a plain return — the
   flexibility is effectively free.
2. **Take the train the day before and sleep at the departure city.** A €80–140 hotel night
   eliminates connection risk on the single highest-stakes travel day of the trip.
3. **Test 10–13 December against 17–20 December.** A documented cheap/expensive boundary sits
   inside their window, and the earlier dates also buy buffer before the Perth anchor.
4. **Book now.** The peak-season sweet spot for Europe–Australia was March–June 2026. At ~3.5–4
   months out they are past the optimum but before the steep part of the curve. **Do not wait for
   Black Friday** — Qatar's 2025 ex-Europe sale ran 21 Nov–2 Dec, base fare only, with Christmas
   blackouts.
5. **Consider a free Abu Dhabi stopover on the return only.** Etihad's promo (**book by 20 December
   2026, travel to end of January 2027**) gives economy **up to 2 free hotel nights** and matches
   their return window almost exactly. Etihad no longer serves Perth, so it cannot help outbound.
6. **If budget dominates over arrival quality, invert:** route via Doha and use Discover Qatar at
   **USD 14 pp/night** for a 4-star. That is a ~10x subsidy against Doha's December market rate and
   the biggest single cost lever available — but it buys a worse jet-lag split and Gulf airspace
   risk, which is exactly why it isn't the recommendation.

## 9. What to verify before booking

**Blocking checks**

1. **Gulf airspace / EASA guidance** at the moment of booking — check
   [ops.group](https://ops.group/blog/middle-east-airspace-current-operational-picture/) and
   [safeairspace.net](https://safeairspace.net/summary/). If still restricted, discount Emirates
   and Qatar for the December outbound regardless of seat quality.
2. **BA A380 upper-deck economy on the specific airframe.** Refurbished aircraft have **zero**
   upper-deck economy. If the seat map doesn't show 2-4-2 in rows 70–83, strategy 3 is dead.
3. **SQ SIN–BCN–MAD frequency** — sources split between 5x weekly (SIA press release, trade
   press) and daily (one frequent-flyer outlet) — and confirm which direction carries the
   Barcelona stop on the chosen day.

**Seat and equipment checks**

4. **Aircraft on SIN–PER** for the chosen day: A350-900 (18") preferred over 787-10 (17.2").
5. **Emirates DXB–PER equipment.** The A380 was pulled in March 2026 for a 777-300ER with a
   July 2026 return planned, and Perth was cut from twice-daily to daily. Sources conflict on the
   current state. A380 = 18.0"; 777-300ER = 17.0".
6. **Seat map at booking, not at check-in.** The upper-deck 2-4-2 pairs and the A380 rear-taper
   pairs are the entire comfort thesis and they go early.
7. **Qantas QF10 LHR–PER nonstop status** for the actual dates — the route has been re-timed or
   re-routed twice in 2026.

**Cost and logistics checks**

8. **Price the stopover both ways** — a 2h connection vs the identical dates as a multi-city with
   a 2-night hub break. The delta is the real stopover cost, and Cathay is known to charge a fee
   on markets outside its current waiver promo.
9. **Etihad's free-stopover promo terms** for the return (book by 20 December 2026, travel to end
   January 2027), and **Turkish's 2026 TourIstanbul / free-hotel terms** — both unverified.
10. **Emirates Dubai Connect window** for economy (6–26h vs 8–26h is reported inconsistently) —
    though the "shortest available connection" rule likely rules it out regardless.
11. **Australian passport UAE stay length** (30 vs 90 days reported inconsistently), if Dubai ends
    up on the itinerary.
12. **Book Renfe AVE / iryo / Ouigo standard, not Avlo** — Avlo's baggage rules are explicitly
    more restrictive, and two people's Australian-summer luggage is already at or over Renfe's
    stated 25 kg / 290 cm allowance.

- All EUR price bands are **synthesised from aggregator averages, not live quotes**. Verify
  against a live multi-city search before acting.
- Kayak route-page "most expensive month" figures are internally inconsistent (one showed
  BCN–PER at €6,122 for October, clearly an artefact) and are used here only as directional
