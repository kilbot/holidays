# Context

Glossary for the Australia trip planner. Terms only — no implementation detail.

## Terms

- **Trip** — the whole journey: leave Valencia in December 2026, return from Australia's east coast in late January–mid February 2027.
- **Traveller** — one of the two people taking the Trip. Costs are usually quoted per couple unless marked per person. Their tastes are **shared, not partitioned** ("we're in this together" — no per-person views anywhere in the site): psytrance festivals, doofs, hippie markets, and alternative culture rate as first-class Event spend candidates for the couple; sport is a minor shared interest (fine as cheap outings, never a big spend or a relocation driver). Paul carries road-trip nostalgia — he hitchhiked Perth→Sydney for NYE 2000 — so a long cross-country drive holds experiential value beyond its cost math.
- **Anchor** — a fixed or semi-fixed date+place commitment the itinerary must honour. Hard anchors: Christmas in Perth (family), New Year's Eve in Sydney. Semi-fixed: the **first days after landing are spent with Paul's dad in Mundaring Hills** ("it'd be rude not to") — doubling as jet-lag recovery and Perth acclimatisation. Soft anchor: Australia Day (26 Jan) — be somewhere good for it, city decided by itinerary flow.
- **Adventure** — (renamed from "capsule", 2026-08-27 — code identifiers may still say `capsule`; migrate opportunistically, never in user-facing copy) a pre-researched, self-contained block of the trip that can be toggled on or off the Plan and moved around the calendar: a destination plus its recommended duration, best-of itinerary, and cost estimate (e.g. "Margaret River, 3 days", "Rottnest, day trip", "Great Barrier Reef, N days from a chosen base").
- **Adventure idea** — a shallow catalog entry: a highly-rated Australian holiday idea captured in a few lines (name, region, rough days, rough cost, why people rate it, season fit) without deep research. The Catalog holds hundreds of these; the Travellers sift them, and survivors get promoted to full Adventures via research.
- **Shortlist state** — every Catalog idea is *unseen*, *interested*, *discarded*, or *placed*. Interested ideas sit visibly on the bench without occupying calendar Days; promotion to a full researched Adventure happens on request. The Catalog is sifted through filters — as many as the data supports (region, tags, season fit, cost, days, taste lens, warnings, locks, nearest airport).
- **Catalog** — the browsable collection of all Adventure ideas, mined in bulk from forums, YouTube, and travel lore. Feeds the site's toggle list; "the more the better" — comprehensiveness beats curation at this tier, since sifting is the Travellers' job.
- **Leg** — a single movement between places (flight, train, ferry, or drive), with a cost and a duration. Legs are **derived, not placed**: the Scheduler computes them from the Adventure sequence and prices them from fare data, with a per-Leg override for mode when the journey IS the experience (e.g. a relocation drive instead of the flight). The long-haul Legs may start with a ground Leg (e.g. train Valencia→Madrid) — the origin is flexible.
- **Plan** — the currently selected set of Adventures and dates, plus the Legs that connect them and the resulting total cost. What the site displays and recalculates as toggles and dates change.
- **Scenario** — a full alternate calendar for a big fork (e.g. "Fireworks NYE" vs "Doof NYE", 12-Feb vs 22-Feb departure): each Scenario has its own Days, Legs, and total cost, compared side by side; exactly one is marked as the current Plan. Small what-ifs don't need a Scenario — they live in Slot benches within a Day.
- **Fork** — a visitor-created Scenario: named, saved, with its own URL (the link IS the permission — no accounts). Forks can never touch the Plan; the Travellers can **adopt** one (copy it into their own Scenario list) and promote it from there. The canonical Plan has a view link (share freely) and an edit link (the couple only).
- **Day** — one calendar day of the Plan, priced individually: its own lodging night, its own activities/Event spend, its own transport. The Plan's cost is the sum of its Days (one Day can cost A$1,000 and the next A$30). Averages are derived display stats, never the pricing mechanism.
- **Scheduler** — the engine that places a toggled Adventure onto the calendar automatically: honouring Locks, inserting Buffer days, preferring cheap feasible weeks. Its placements are proposals — the Travellers drag to adjust; it never has the final word.
- **Warning** — the only enforcement mechanism in the site: a violated Lock, missing Buffer, jam-packed week, Daily-cap blowout, or crossed Budget ceiling shows as a badge on the offending Days and lines. Nothing is ever blocked or refused — the site informs, the Travellers decide.
- **Slot** — a position in a Day that holds swappable alternatives: one active option plus a visible bench of candidates (with their costs) that can be swapped in. A Day can have multiple Slots. The Plan is built by swapping, not by committing.
- **Lock** — an Adventure's scheduling constraint: **flexible** (any day), **window-locked** (best within a date range, e.g. reef 18–31 Jan), **weekday-locked** (only certain days of the week, e.g. Salamanca Market on Saturdays, a 3×-weekly flight), or **date-locked** (fixed dates: NYE, a festival). The scheduler honours Locks; everything else floats.
- **Buffer day** — a deliberately unscheduled Day: chill time, recovery, or the freedom to stay longer somewhere fun or leave somewhere disappointing early. Buffers are first-class, not leftover slack — the Plan should refuse to be jam-packed, capsules are never scheduled edge to edge, and Buffer days still cost money (lodging + food wherever they land).
- **Budget** — the money frame the Plan is judged against: €6,000–€10,000 per Traveller (€12,000–€20,000 for the couple). Displayed in EUR; Australian costs are converted from AUD.
- **Daily cap** — the hard ceiling on a paid day's living costs (lodging + food + local transport): **A$500 per couple**. A ceiling, not a target: day-to-day the aim is **cheapest possible** (cheap Airbnb, motels, hostels — and a tent where it makes sense: "we'll camp if we have to"), with occasional deliberate exceptions (an A$150–200/night hotel where it's warranted). The Budget itself is also a ceiling, never a spending goal.
- **Event spend** — the deliberate splurge category the day-to-day thrift pays for: reef boat days, NYE, festivals, marquee experiences, inter-city Legs. Always separate Plan line items, never averaged into daily costs. Money saved on living goes here.
- **Home base** — a place with free lodging and a borrowed car (Paul's dad in Mundaring Hills, the Perth family home, the sister's farm). Adventures near a Home base cost far less than east-coast Adventures, where hotels and car rental are paid.
- **Fare snapshot** — a stored result of flight-price research for a specific route and date range, with a fetched-at date. The fallback data source if live pricing is too expensive.
- **Live pricing** — fetching current fares from a flight-data API at the moment the user changes dates or toggles an Adventure. Preferred if affordable.
- **Date coverage** — which route/day pairs already hold a fare, and therefore which days are free to browse. Every day of the trip window is choosable on a flight search (user, 2026-08-27, #61: "we need every day in December, January and February — a calendar"), and the honest half of that is saying which ones cost nothing: a **known** day has a stored price and is instant, a **warm** day is on the weekly cron's list, and a **cold** day has never been priced. A cold day used to state its price in calls and wait for an explicit go; the Travellers have since settled that trade in favour of spending (user, 2026-08-27, #68: *"just make the calls; we're going to use the data until it is gone"*), so a cold day is now fetched on selection like any other and the transparency moved from a gate to a report — the page says how many origins it is pricing while it prices them. The two ceilings that can still refuse a call are the **monthly budget** (the plan the Travellers pay for) and the **daily guard** (runaway protection, not budgeting), and when either is what is holding prices back the page says so in words rather than quietly degrading to stored ones. The same "informs, never blocks" bargain as a Warning or a Constraint, applied to the site's own metered API: a cold day is never refused, and a degraded one is never silent.
- **No Middle East transits** — hard constraint (user, 2026-08-27, "in today's climate"): itineraries transiting Gulf/Middle East hubs (DOH, DXB, AUH, and kin) are excluded by default, not merely penalized. Asia-routed options (SIN, HKG, KUL) are the lane. **Istanbul is not a Middle East transit** for this rule (user, 2026-08-27, #60): IST is an ordinary via-point and ranks normally, which matters because Turkish is the only carrier ending the return at Valencia's own airport. Excluded is not deleted — see Constraint.
- **Flight price ceiling** — hard constraint (user, 2026-08-27): **€1,000 per person** for a long-haul crossing to or from Australia, measured across the whole journey (the long-haul, the positioning move into the hub, its bags, the night before, the ride home from the arrival airport, and taxes). A ceiling, not a target, and long-haul only — Australian domestic Legs have their own bands. Like the Middle East rule, it is a default position rather than a fact: it is the starting value of a control the Travellers can move.
- **Constraint** — a Traveller-set rule that decides what a list shows: the Middle East exclusion, the flight price ceiling. Constraints are **visible and adjustable, never baked in** — each is a labelled control on the page it filters, with a plain-words sentence saying what is currently true, and what it catches stays on the page greyed out, carrying the reason it was caught. The site never deletes an option to enforce a rule; a rule nobody can see reads as the world being that shape, and anyone looking at the list has to be able to tell why it looks the way it does. The same "informs, never blocks" bargain as a Warning, applied to the Travellers' own rules.
- **Fare floor** — the cheapest result in a flight search with every Constraint switched off: a benchmark, never a recommendation. It is routinely a Gulf or Chinese routing with a forty-hour elapsed time ("that's the cheapest it can possibly be, then we see how close we can get" — user, 2026-08-27). Every ranked option shows its distance from the floor, which is what the comfort, the protection and the saved hours actually cost.
- **Comfort-first** — the flight-selection criterion for long-haul Legs: best comfort per euro, not cheapest. Signals: aircraft type (e.g. A380), seat position, layover airport quality (Singapore Changi preferred), option of an overnight stopover.
- **Evidence label** — how much of a comfort-score component is evidence, stated on the component itself (user challenge, 2026-08-27, #69: *"is it empirical… or are you just pulling it out of your ass?"*). Three: **measured** (a controlled study found the effect — the seat's inches, and cabin altitude, which is the best-evidenced variable in the literature and was the one the formula was missing), **rated** (a third-party rating or expert aggregate — every airline score here, and no published study validates any of them against measured comfort), **judgment** (no literature located; our call, said out loud — three of the four adjustments). Labels ride on the numbers in a row's derivation with the audit's own sentence behind them, and they are typographic, never coloured: the four signal colours mean things about the trip, and epistemics are not a status. Full audit in `docs/research/comfort-ratings.md`, section "Evidence basis".
- **Pin** — a fare the Travellers marked as worth remembering: the quote as it stood (route, day, carrier, price, comfort score) with the instant it was taken. A dated observation rather than a bookmark to a row — the search re-ranks, and the day is half of what gets forgotten (user, 2026-08-27, #68: *"sometimes you find a flight you think is good, but then you forget which day it was"*). Pins live in the Plan document rather than in a browser, because the trip has two Travellers and two phones.
- **Watchlist** — the pins, above the search they came from, each showing what its price has done since: the quote at pin time against the newest price **already stored**, with the difference as a chip and the stored prices as a small line over time. It never fetches a fare — twenty pins refreshing themselves on every page load would spend the month in a week — so a pinned day nobody has priced since says exactly that. Capped at twenty, which refuses rather than evicts: dropping the oldest would silently delete something a Traveller chose to watch.
- **Comfort weight** — how much of the comfort score is the airline's ratings and how much is the seat the itinerary actually flies. Defaults to the research's 0.55/0.45 and moves inside **0.30–0.70**, the bracket the published evidence leaves open: physical-comfort studies put the aeroplane first, satisfaction studies put the airline first, and they are measuring the same spectrum. Treated as a Constraint rather than a fact — a labelled slider on the Flights page, re-ranking live, with a sentence saying what it is set to and whether the top of the search depends on it. For this corpus it does not: Singapore Airlines leads on both axes and wins at every setting, so the weight rearranges the middle of the table.
